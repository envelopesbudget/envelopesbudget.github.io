Credit cards and debt

A credit card, a car loan, a debt you had before the app. How to set them up, how to pay so the budget doesn't lie, and how to watch the debt shrink.

Credit card

There's one rule: a purchase on a credit card spends envelope money just like a purchase on a debit card. The difference is where that money waits: not in an account but in the credit card's category, until you pay the bank.

How to set it up

  1. “Accounts” → “⋮” → “Add an account”.

  2. Type — “Credit card”. Name — whatever you call the card.

  3. Amount — how much you owe the bank right now. Type it and tap “±” to make it negative.

  4. Save. The card goes to the “Credit cards” section, and on “Budget” a category with the same name appears in the “Card payments” group.

Leave that category alone: the app uses it to count the debt of this particular card. More in the article “Accounts”.

A purchase

Record it as usual: account — the credit card, category — what you bought. You bought groceries for $35 — “Groceries” got $35 thinner, and the same $35 moved into the credit card's category for the payment. You don't need to set anything aside yourself.

Debt you had before you started

The starting debt — $800 on the card from last year — isn't set aside by the app: there was never money for it in the envelopes.

Set money aside for it in the credit card's category, like any envelope: on “Budget”, tap its amount and enter what you can manage this month. All at once or a little at a time — your call. Until you set it aside, there's no money for paying it off.

The yellow minus

You spent more on the credit card than the envelope had — the amount turned yellow, with a card icon. Red, with a wallet icon, is a minus from your own money.

Red eats your money

A minus on a debit card or cash. At the end of the month it reduces next month's “unassigned”.

Yellow grows your debt

You didn't spend money — you borrowed it. Only the covered part went into the credit card's category; for the rest, there's no money for the payment.

What to do

Cover a yellow minus like a red one: tap the amount → “Cover from another category” → “Take …” from another envelope. Then the money for the payment is topped up automatically. Couldn't do it? Next month, put the difference straight into the credit card's category.

Paying the card

How much to pay the bank is shown in the credit card's category on “Budget”: that much is already set aside for the payment.

  1. “+ Transaction”, account — your debit card.

  2. “Payee” → the “Transfers” section → your credit card. The form header turns gray, and no category is needed.

  3. Amount — how much you're paying. Save.

The money on your debit card goes down, so does the credit card debt, and with it the amount set aside in the credit card's category. The envelopes don't change: you did the spending earlier, when you made the purchases. More in the article “Transaction form”.

Pay it all or the minimum payment

The credit card's category holds exactly as much as you spent on the card from your envelopes. Pay everything set aside each month, and no new debt builds up. Pay only the minimum, and the rest stays in the category, waiting; the bank charges interest on the remaining debt.

Card interest and fees

The bank charged interest or a service fee — that's spending.

Record an expense: account — the credit card, payee — the bank, category — one of its own, for example “Interest and fees”. The envelope shrinks, and the money for the payment is set aside, as with any purchase. It shows in the reports too: what this credit costs you.

Loan

A car loan, a mortgage, an installment plan — money you've already spent and are paying back in parts.

How to set it up

  1. “Accounts” → “⋮” → “Add an account”.

  2. Type — “Loan”. Amount — how much is left to repay according to the bank statement, with a minus sign (“±”).

  3. The account goes to the “Debts” section. It's off budget: this money has no envelopes, and the budget doesn't touch the starting debt.

An envelope for the payment

Create a category for the monthly payment — for example, “Loans” in the “Fixed expenses” group — and a “Monthly” goal for the payment amount. Every month the goal reminds you to set the money aside. More in the article “Goals”.

Paying a loan

  1. “+ Transaction”, account — the card you pay from.

  2. “Payee” → “Transfers” → the loan account.

  3. The loan is off budget, so the app asks for a category: choose “Loans”. The money leaves the envelope, and the debt on the loan account goes down.

The bank says $80 of the payment is interest

Record the whole payment as a single transfer, as above. Then once a month, record the interest on the loan account itself: “Outgoing”, account — the loan, payee — the bank, amount — the interest. No category needed: the account is off budget. Then the debt in the app matches the bank, and the “Loans” envelope gives up exactly your payment.

A payment reminder

Create a scheduled payment as a transfer: “Settings” → “Scheduled payments” → “+ Payment”, “Payee” → “Transfers” → the loan account, category “Loans”, “Repeat” — “Monthly”. The payment appears on both accounts' screens, a reminder arrives in advance, and “Confirm” records the transfer. The same goes for a credit card payment: from your debit card to the credit card. More in the article “Scheduled payments”.

Watching the debt

WhereWhat it tells you
“Accounts”The “Credit cards” and “Debts” sections — how much you owe right now.
“Budget” → “Card payments”How much is set aside to pay off each card.
“Reports” → “Card debt”The debt on all credit cards month by month: is it shrinking or growing. Available if you have a credit card.
“Reports” → “Net worth”Everything you own minus all debts. Every loan payment moves the line up.

More about the reports in the article “Reports”.
