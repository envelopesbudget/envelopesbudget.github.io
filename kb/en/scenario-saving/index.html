Saving up for something big

A laptop, a renovation, a car, a down payment on an apartment. How to set aside a little at a time, see whether you'll make it by the deadline, and not spend the savings by accident.

“Budget”, “Goals”, the category screen

An envelope just for the purchase

Money for a laptop mixed in with money for living gets spent first. A separate envelope protects it: while it sits in “Laptop”, it doesn't show up in “Groceries”.

  1. On “Budget”, find the group that holds “Big purchases” and “Travel & vacation”.

  2. Tap “⊕” next to its name — a new category. Name it after the purchase: “Laptop”.

  3. Or save in the ready-made “Big purchases” if there's only one purchase.

More in the article “Budget”.

A “By date” goal

Tell the app how much and by which month — it works out how much to put in each month.

  1. Tap the category on “Budget” → the “Goal” card → “Add a goal”.

  2. “Goal type” — “By date”.

  3. “Amount” — the price of the purchase, “Save up by month” — when you need it.

  4. “Save”.

A $2,400 laptop in six months — the goal will ask for $400 a month. Miss a month, and the shortfall is spread over the remaining ones, so the payment grows. Put in more, and the next ones shrink. More in the article “Goals”.

Saving without a deadline

A “Balance” goal: the envelope should hold this amount, and when doesn't matter. It suits “a new car someday” and an emergency cushion. More in the article “Goals”.

Am I on track

A quick estimate on the category screen

Tap the category — a “By date” goal has a switch under the bar: “By amount” and “By date”. Move “Save per month” — you see which month you'll get there. Move the month — you see how much to put in. Like the new date? — “Make it the goal date: …”. More in the article “Goals”.

Short this month

On “Budget”, under the amount there's a flag and “… to go”. The “Underfunded” chip above the list leaves only those categories. On “Overview”, the shortfall shows in the “Goals” and “What’s ahead” blocks.

Where the money is

Savings are often kept in a separate account: a savings account, a deposit, a piggy bank. How you set up that account decides whether the money stays in the envelope.

On budget — for a purchase

An account of the “Bank account” type. You moved $400 from checking to savings — the “Laptop” envelope didn't change: the money moved, but it's still yours and still in the budget. The goal sees it and doesn't ask again. Saving for a purchase? Choose this option.

Off budget — for wealth

An account of the “Tracking” type: investments, a retirement deposit. The app records a transfer there as money leaving an envelope — it asks for a category, and the envelope empties. This won't work for a purchase: the goal will ask for money again, even though you have it.

More in the article “Accounts”.

Several goals at once

A vacation, a laptop and holiday gifts — there may not be enough money for everything this month.

“Budget” → “⋮” → “Assign to goals”. The app spreads “unassigned” across all goals at once. Not enough for everything? Goals get money in order, top to bottom, as the categories are arranged on “Budget”. Keep what matters higher up: “⋮” → “Change the order”. More in the article “Goals”.

The purchase

  1. Record the purchase as usual — in the “Laptop” category. The envelope empties — that's what you were saving for.

  2. It came out cheaper? The rest stays in the envelope. Need it elsewhere? On “Budget”, tap the “Laptop” amount and enter less: the difference goes back to “unassigned”.

  3. It came out more expensive? The amount turns red. Tap it → “Take …” from another envelope.

  4. Don't need the goal anymore? “Budget” → “⋮” → “Goals” → tap the category → “Delete”. The money in the envelope stays. The category with the purchase itself can't be deleted — only hidden.

Bought it with a credit card

That works too: the money from “Laptop” is set aside for paying off the card. Bought it on credit or in installments? That's a debt; how to track it is in the scenario “Credit cards and debt”.

How much I save

The “Pay yourself” block on “Overview” shows what percent of your income you set aside — for the month, three months and the year. The norm is 10%. Choose which categories count as “saved” in “Settings → Budget → Pay yourself”: “Big purchases”, “Laptop”, “Rainy day fund”. More in the article “Overview”.
