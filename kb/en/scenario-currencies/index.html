Several currencies and the exotic

Your salary in one currency, savings in another, a little bitcoin and trips abroad. What the app can do with currencies, where the limits are, and how to keep your books so the envelopes don't lie.

“Settings” → “Budget currency”, off-budget accounts, a transfer with a rate

The main rule

A budget has one currency. All envelopes, all budget accounts and “unassigned” are in it. Other currencies live in accounts off budget: you see how much is there, but that money isn't divided among the envelopes.

Why it works this way

An envelope answers “how much can I spend on food”. If it holds both dollars and euros, the answer changes every day along with the exchange rate. One currency in the envelopes — and the answer is always the same.

Budget currency

“Settings” → “Budget” → “Budget currency”. The list has sixty currencies, the current one in the first row, with a search by name and symbol at the top: “zloty” finds “Złoty”.

Only the symbol changes — the app doesn't convert amounts. If you kept your budget in dollars and pick euros, $1,000 becomes €1,000. That's why you choose the currency at the start, not in the middle of your bookkeeping.

A budget for each currency

Each budget has its own currency. Living between two countries? You can keep two budgets: “Home” in dollars and “Berlin” in euros. Tap the budget name in the “Overview” header — there's the budget switcher and a new budget; you'll be asked for the currency when creating it.

An account in another currency

  1. “Accounts” → “⋮” → “Add an account”.

  2. Type — “Tracking” (or “Loan” if it's a debt). Only an off-budget account can have its own currency.

  3. In the “Account currency” section, tap the “Currency” field — a list of sixty currencies opens, the same as for the budget: the current one in the first row, a search by name and symbol at the top. Names are in the country's own language: “zloty” finds “Złoty”.

  4. Cryptocurrency — chips under the field: ₿, USDT, USDC.

  5. Not there? Type it yourself in “Or type your own (ticker)”: ETH, a stock or fund ticker. The account's amounts will be labeled with it.

  6. Amount — how much is in the account in that currency.

The account goes to the “Off budget” section, with amounts in its own symbol: “€1,200.00”. More in the article “Accounts”.

Exchange

Bought euros with dollars? That's a transfer between your own accounts, just with a rate.

  1. “+ Transaction”, account — your dollar card, “Payee” → “Transfers” → the euro account.

  2. Amount — how many dollars left.

  3. Under the payee are “Credited” and “Rate”. Enter either the rate or how many euros arrived — the other is calculated. The button next to the rate flips its direction: dollars per one euro or euros per one dollar.

  4. The euro account is off budget — the app asks for a category: which envelope the money left. For example, “Rainy day fund” or “Investments”.

More in the article “Transaction form”.

Sold the euros back

A transfer from the euro account to your card. The “Category” field opens “Where to put the income”: “Income for …” — if it's new money to hand out, “To an expense category…” — if it's returning to the envelope it left.

Living in one country, saving in another

The budget is in the currency you're paid in and spend every day: dollars, euros, pounds. Money back home, a deposit in another currency, dollars in a “rainy day” stash — off-budget accounts, each in its own currency. The envelopes count what you live on, and the rest is visible next to them on “Accounts”.

Record card spending abroad as the amount the bank charged in your card's currency. The amount in local currency, if you need it, goes in the note. More in the scenario “A trip with friends”.

Cryptocurrency

An off-budget account, the ₿, USDT or USDC chip under the “Currency” field — and the “Cryptocurrency” switch turns on by itself: “8 decimal places”. For your own ticker (ETH, SOL), turn it on by hand. Picked a regular currency from the list? The switch turns off.

The number of decimal places is chosen once: once the account has transactions, the switch says “Cannot change: the account has transactions”.

Currencies without cents

The yen, won and dong have no minor units, but the app still shows “.00”. It's only the display: type whole numbers as usual. With “With a decimal point” entry, 1500 is 1,500, not 15.00. How amounts are typed — “Settings” → “Language and formats” → “Amount entry”.

How amounts are written

“Settings” → “Language and formats” → “Amount format”: the thousands and decimal separators, the currency symbol on the left or right. Out of the box it follows the interface language: in English, “$1,234.56”. English on a phone from India gets lakhs and crores right away: “₹12,34,567.89”. More in the article “Settings”.

What's missing

  • Exchange rates from the internet. You enter the exchange rate yourself, when you exchange. That's also protection: the app doesn't ask any service for rates, so nobody outside learns which currencies your money is in. Your data stays with you — as the privacy policy says too.

  • Conversion into one currency. “Net worth” adds up only accounts in the budget currency: a euro account isn't included. On “Accounts”, a section that has an account in another currency shows no total — there's no way to add dollars to euros.

  • Budget accounts in another currency. Why — above.
