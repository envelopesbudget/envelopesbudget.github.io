Two or more people, one budget

A family with shared money: both pay, both record, both see how much is left in each envelope. How to agree with the app and with each other so there's no confusion and no duplicates.

“Settings”, “Sync” section

Who it's for

The money is shared: paychecks are pooled, and rent, food and vacations come from the common pot. Each person pays with their own card or cash, but the envelopes are shared: both see “Groceries”, and whoever hits the limit first is the one who sees red.

If each of you also has personal money the other shouldn't see, see the scenario “Couples: shared costs and personal money”.

Setup

Technically this is the same budget on several devices — the steps are in the scenario “One budget on several devices”. There's one difference, and it's important.

A separate cloud account — just for the budget

All devices must sign in to the cloud with one account. But signing in to the husband's personal Google account on the wife's phone is a bad idea: the phone will start syncing his contacts, photos and email.

Create a new Google account — just for the budget — and use it to sign in to the app's cloud on every device. Everyone keeps their own personal accounts. Same for Dropbox: a separate Dropbox, signed in with a username and password.

One cloud password for everyone

If you set a password, set the same one on every phone. The password doesn't sync: it's entered on each device by hand.

Who paid for what

The app doesn't remember which phone a transaction was recorded on. The account is what tells spending apart.

An account for each person

Give each person their own accounts: “Olivia's card”, “Peter's card”, “Olivia's cash”. Whichever account a purchase is recorded on, that person paid. Each of you reconciles account balances with your own bank — “Balance reconciliation” in the article “Accounts”.

Whose spending this month — “Activity” → “⋮” → “Filter”, filter by account.

If you need “who owes whom”

In a shared budget you usually don't: the money is shared anyway. But if you've agreed to split something in half — a tag with “Settle up” turned on for those purchases shows who owes whom how much. More in the article “Payees and tags”.

Assigning money

Dividing a paycheck among envelopes is easier for one person — or for two at one screen.

Why not each from your own phone

If both of you change how much is set aside in the same category at the same time, whoever's edit reached the cloud last wins — or the app shows a “Conflict” and asks you to choose. The amount in the envelope may not be the one you agreed on.

Agree on who does the assigning. Before you start, open the app and give it a couple of seconds to sync — so you see the other person's latest spending.

What both see

“Unassigned”, the balance of each envelope, “Overspent” and “Running out” on “Overview” are shared. If “Groceries” turns yellow, both see it, and you're discussing the same numbers.

Scheduled payments

Rent and subscriptions are shared, and the due date arrives on both phones.

One person confirms

If two people tap “Confirm” on the same payment before the phones have synced, the transaction is recorded twice. Agree on who confirms scheduled payments, or let the app sync before confirming. Delete the extra transaction in “Activity”.

Due date reminders arrive on both phones — for whoever has them turned on. Each person sets them up on their own phone: “Settings” → “Reminders”.

Every day

Recorded something? Sync

The app syncs when you open it, not when you minimize it. Recorded a big purchase and know the other person is about to look at the budget? Tap the sync icon on “Overview”.

The evening reminder counts you both

“Remind me to add spending” stays quiet if the budget already has spending today — including spending entered by the other person and brought in by sync. If you want it to remind each of you, each should record their own spending the same day.

“Recent transactions” are shared

On “Overview” you see what the other person bought: their purchases appear there after a sync.

Common mistakes

What happenedWhy, and what to do
One phone has more spending than the otherThe phones signed in to the cloud with different accounts — that's two different clouds. Sign in everywhere with one, separate account for the budget.
A transaction appeared twiceBoth of you entered it: for example, a shared receipt was recorded both by the one who paid and by the one who carried the bags. Agree: whoever's card it was records it.
A scheduled payment was recorded twiceBoth tapped “Confirm”. Delete the extra transaction; from now on one person confirms.
“The cloud is password-protected” on one phoneOne phone has the password set, and the other has none or a different one. Set the same one.
The currency symbol differs between the two of youThe budget currency lives on the device. Fix it in “Settings” → “Budget currency”.
