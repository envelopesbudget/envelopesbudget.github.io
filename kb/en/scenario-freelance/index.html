Freelance and irregular income

Money comes in when the client pays: feast one month, famine the next. How to stop living from one payment to the next with an income like that, set money aside for taxes, and know what you really earn.

The big idea

With a salary, the month is predictable: paid on the 5th, enough until the next 5th. A freelancer doesn't know the month's income in advance — so you can't plan from it. Plan from the money you already have.

Spend what you earned earlier

The month lives on what came in before. What comes in now works for the next month. How.

Smooth it out with a cushion

A good month tops up the reserve, a lean month draws on it. How.

Set the tax aside right away

A percentage of each payment goes into its own envelope before the money gets spent. How.

Spend last month's money

Record each payment not to this month but to the next one. Then by the 1st, the whole month is already paid for with money you've earned, and you divide it among the envelopes once — not in pieces after every payment.

How to record a payment

  1. “+ Transaction” → “+ Incoming”, payee — the client, amount.

  2. Tap the “Category” field — “Where to put the income” opens.

  3. Choose “Income for December” instead of “Income for November”. Save.

The transaction date stays the real one. Only the budget month the money shows up in changes.

Where to start

For the first month you'll have to both spend what you have and save for the next one. Start with a payment you haven't assigned yet. Until you have enough for a month ahead, put at least part of it into next month: the app shows how much is already there in “Month budget” on “Overview” — the “December budget” line.

The goal is an “Age of money” of 30 days or more: it means you're spending last month's money. More in the article “Overview”.

A cushion for lean months

Even with money a month ahead, two lean months in a row happen. A cushion is an envelope that gets you through them.

How to set it up

  1. The “Rainy day fund” category — or one of your own, “Cushion”.

  2. A “Balance” goal: the amount is your usual spending for 3–6 months. How much that is — “Average income per month” and “Spending trend”, below.

  3. In a good month, put everything above the usual into the cushion.

The “Balance” goal makes sure the reserve doesn't melt away: spend part of it, and the app asks you to put it back. More in the article “Goals”.

A lean month

There wasn't enough for the envelopes, and spending went into the red. Tap the red amount on “Budget” → “Cover from another category” → on the “Rainy day fund” row, “Take …”. The money moves from the cushion to where the minus is. More in the article “Budget”.

Taxes — straight into their own envelope

The tax on a payment is easy to spend: the money is on the card, and the tax is due next quarter.

A “% income” goal

  1. The “Taxes” category.

  2. A “% income” goal — the percentage you pay. Under the percentage, leave “From this month's income”: tax is calculated on the money that came in, not on the average.

  3. Each payment updates the goal by itself: more came in, the goal went up, and “… to go” tells you how much to add.

What you set aside builds up in the envelope until the payment date: a plus carries over from month to month. More in the article “Goals”.

If you record income to next month

Then this month's “% income” goal is known on the 1st: the month's income arrived last month. Handy — the tax is set aside first thing, along with the other envelopes.

Money came in — divide it up in one tap

Dividing up each payment by hand gets tiring. The “By percentage” auto-assign does it for you.

Set it up once

  1. On “Budget”, tap “unassigned” in the header — “Auto-assign the budget” opens.

  2. “By percentage” → “Set percentages”: for example, “Taxes” — 5%, “Rainy day fund” — 20%, “Investments” — 10%.

  3. The percentages are remembered. Whatever isn't covered by a percentage stays in “unassigned” — assign it to the month's envelopes yourself.

From then on: money came in — tap “unassigned” → “By percentage”. The app divides up exactly what's unassigned right now.

Why percentages and not amounts

With an irregular income, a fixed amount is impossible one month and laughable the next. A percentage rises and falls along with your income. Rounding — the “To whole”, “To 10”, “To 100” chips on the same sheet.

Clients and projects

The client is the payee

Record every payment with the client in the “Payee” field. Tap the client in “Settings” → “Payees” — their screen opens: “Income trends” shows how much they pay on average, and the “Year” period shows all their payments for the year. You can see who feeds you and who pays twice a year. More in the article “Payees and tags”.

The project is a tag

Create a tag for the project: “cafe website”. Put it on both the client's payment and the project's costs: a service subscription, a taxi to a meeting, a font. The tag screen shows “Spent”, “Received” and “Net” — what the project really brought in. More in the article “Payees and tags”.

Expected payments

The invoice is out, and the money was promised by the 15th. To not forget and to see what's coming, create a scheduled payment.

  1. “Settings” → “Scheduled payments” → “+ Payment”.

  2. “+ Incoming”, the client, the amount, the date payment was promised, “Repeat” — “Once”.

  3. The payment appears in “What’s ahead” and in the “Calendar” on “Overview”.

  4. The money arrived on time — “Confirm” on the confirmation screen. Earlier than due — swipe the payment row left — “Enter”. The client is late — open the payment and change the “Date” field.

Until the payment is confirmed, it isn't in the budget: envelopes don't get filled with promised money. More in the article “Scheduled payments”.

Keep work money separate

If your work has its own account and its own expenses — services, equipment, an accountant — you can keep them in a second budget, for example “Work”. Tap the budget name in the “Overview” header — there's the budget switcher and a new budget.

The work budget holds payments and work expenses. What you take for living goes as a transfer to your personal card: in the work budget it's spending, in the personal one it's income. That way your personal budget lives on an even amount every month, and all the ups and downs stay in the work budget.
