"Couples: shared costs and personal money"

Each of you has your own salary and your own spending, while the apartment, food and vacations are split in half. How to handle this in the app today, what it can't do yet, and how to square up once a month.

What works today

What you'd like is this: a shared budget, “Our home”, that both of you keep, plus a personal one each that the other can't see. Through one shared cloud that won't work — why is below. But it will work if you pass the shared budget over Wi-Fi at home.

Why

A phone signs in to the cloud with one account, and every budget on that phone syncs to that cloud. The cloud password is also one per phone, not per budget. So a shared budget in a shared cloud and a personal one hidden from your partner can't live on the same phone: the personal one would go to the same place as the shared one.

Three approaches work.

Personal budgets and a “shared” tag

Each has their own budget — nobody sees the other's money. Shared spending is marked with a tag, and once a month you compare and even out. Details — below.

Two budgets, the shared one over Wi-Fi

Each phone has its own budget and the shared “Our home”. Each of you has your own cloud, and you pass the shared budget to each other over Wi-Fi at home. Details — below.

One shared budget for both

All the money in one budget, personal money as its own categories. Both see everything. Good if you have nothing to hide from each other. Details — below.

The “shared” tag approach

Each has their own budget on their own phone: their own cloud, or no cloud at all. Each records shared spending in their own budget as usual and puts a tag on it.

How to set it up — each on your own phone

  1. Create a category for your share of shared spending — for example, “Family” in the “Fixed expenses” group. Every month, put in as much as you agreed to contribute to the shared pot.

  2. Paid for something shared — groceries for home, utilities? Record the purchase in this category.

  3. In the “Tags” field, create a “shared” tag and put it on every such purchase.

  4. On “Overview”, in the “My tags” block → “⋮” → “Choose tags…”, check “shared”, and set the period to “Total for this month”.

Why one category and not “Groceries” and “Utilities”

Your personal budget answers the question “how much do I spend”. Your share of the shared home is one line item, like rent: “Family”. If you want to see where the shared money goes, open the “shared” tag screen: it has “By category”. In that case, put shared spending in the regular categories, and the tag will gather it together.

End of the month: who owes whom

  1. Each of you checks in “My tags” how much you spent on “shared” this month.

  2. Add the two amounts and split in half — that's each person's share.

  3. Whoever spent less than their share sends the difference to the other.

Example. Olivia spent $1,200 on shared costs, Peter spent $800. That's $2,000 in total, so each share is $1,000. Peter sends Olivia $200.

How to record the transfer

  • Peter — a $200 expense in the “Family” category with the “shared” tag: it's also his contribution to the shared pot.

  • Olivia — $200 coming in from Peter to the same “Family” category, with the same tag: the money returned to the envelope. How to record a refund to a category — in the article “Transaction form”.

In Peter's “My tags” it becomes $1,000. In Olivia's it stays $1,200: the block counts only spending. Her $1,000 is on the “shared” tag screen: “Received” $200 and “Net” $1,000.

Compare using “My tags” before the transfer — after it, the numbers are already evened out.

What about “Settle up” on the tag?

“Settle up” calculates “spent minus received” in your budget — it's good when you split a cost with someone who doesn't write to your budget: a trip with friends, a group gift. For a couple with two budgets it shows only your half of the picture: the other person's contribution isn't in your budget. So here, compare using “My tags”. About “Settle up” — in the article “Payees and tags”.

Two budgets, the shared one over Wi-Fi

Each phone has two budgets: your own and the shared “Our home”. Each of you has your own cloud — your own account, your own password. Both budgets go to the phone owner's cloud, and the other person doesn't see them there. You pass only “Our home” to each other — over Wi-Fi, when you're both at home.

How to set it up

  1. Each of you connects a cloud on your own phone with your own account, as usual.

  2. Olivia creates the “Our home” budget: tap the budget name in the “Overview” header — there's the list of budgets and a new budget. Peter creates an empty budget with the same name on his phone.

  3. The first time, Peter receives “Our home” from Olivia over Wi-Fi (how — below) and chooses “Replace completely”: his empty budget becomes a copy of Olivia's.

Exchanging at home

  1. Both of you open “Our home”. The exchange happens between the open budgets.

  2. “Settings” → “Local transfer”. Olivia: “Start the server”. Peter: “Scan the QR” → “Receive the data” → “Merge”.

  3. Olivia: “Stop the server”.

The exchange goes both ways: Peter gets Olivia's edits, and his edits go to Olivia — transactions and receipt photos alike. It doesn't matter which of you sends and which receives.

“This looks like a different budget” — tap “Cancel”. If one of you has your own budget open instead of “Our home”, the two budgets share no accounts or transactions, and the app asks before merging. “Continue anyway” would pour personal spending into the shared budget, and there'd be no way to separate it again.

What if the budgets did merge?

You can't separate them by hand, but you can roll both back to an automatic backup — the app makes these itself, every 5 days by default. The exchange goes both ways, so two budgets are spoiled: Peter's personal one (“Our home” came into it), and Olivia's “Our home” (Peter's personal spending came into it). Each fixes their own on their own phone.

  1. Open the spoiled budget. “Settings” → “Backups”.

  2. Find a backup of this budget (its name is in the backup's row) dated before the exchange. Tap the clock-with-arrow icon → “Restore”. After that, don't tap “Sync now”: the merged budget is still in the cloud.

  3. Delete this budget's sync file in your cloud — as in the article “Cloud and backups”, steps 2–4. Each of you has your own cloud, and only your phone writes this budget there, so the part about several devices doesn't apply.

  4. Edits made after the backup will be lost. Shared ones can be recovered: once Olivia has rolled back “Our home”, exchange once more with both of you on “Our home”, and the shared edits return to her from Peter's “Our home” — his copy is clean. Personal spending entered after the backup will have to be recorded again.

Before the first exchange it's worth making backups by hand — each on your own phone, and of both budgets: a backup is made for the open budget. Open your own budget → “Backups” → “Create a backup now”, then open “Our home” and repeat. That way you'd roll back not to a five-day-old automatic backup but to the state right before the exchange.

Does the cloud password have to match?

No. The Wi-Fi transfer is encrypted with the confirmation code from the sending phone's screen; the backup password plays no part in it. The password in the “Cloud encryption” block locks only your cloud. It has to be the same only when two phones write to one cloud — here the clouds are different.

What if you edited away from home?

Each of you keeps “Our home” on your own phone: record a purchase in the store, and it's immediately in your copy and your cloud. Exchange at home — and both have everything. If one phone still has an older version of the app, the receiver's edits won't go back — the app says so; then exchange again, swapping roles. If you both edited the same transaction, the later edit stays, as with regular sync.

The trade-off: the shared budget is in sync only once you're both home and have exchanged.

One shared budget for both

If there are no personal secrets, it's simplest to keep everything together — then you see the whole picture and a single “unassigned” for both of you.

  • Setup — as in the scenario “Two or more people, one budget”: a separate cloud account for the budget, signed in on both phones.

  • Personal money — as categories of its own: “Olivia's personal”, “Peter's personal”. Each month the agreed amount goes into them; how to spend it is up to the owner.

  • Accounts — each has their own: “Olivia's card”, “Peter's card”. That way you see who paid.

The trade-off: both of you see everything — spending from the “personal” categories and card balances alike.

What's not there yet

A shared budget that syncs through the cloud for both of you on its own, alongside personal ones hidden from your partner, and moving a shared expense from a personal budget to the shared one in one tap — not there yet. We'll build this when people need it. If that's you, write to “Telegram chat” (“Settings”, “Help & contact” section): the more such messages, the sooner.
