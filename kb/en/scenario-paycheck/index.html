Paycheck to paycheck

The money runs out before the month does, and payday is still a week away. How to divide up what you have, make it to the next paycheck without red, and step by step get out of the rat race.

The big idea

Envelopes don't give you more money than you have. But they show you in advance whether it'll last until payday — not on the last day, but on the first.

Assign only what you have

An envelope is filled with money that's already in your accounts. A promised paycheck isn't money yet. How.

Essentials first

Housing, food, getting to work, medicine, a loan payment. Everything else comes after. How.

A minus isn't failure

Move money from another envelope and carry on. How.

Payday — divide it up right away

Until money is divided up, it feels like a lot. Divided into envelopes, it tells the truth: $200 for food until the 5th, and that's it.

In order

  1. Open “Budget”. At the top is “unassigned”: that much money still has no job.

  2. First fill the envelopes you can't get to the next paycheck without: rent, utilities, “Groceries”, getting to work, medicine, the minimum loan payment.

  3. Then everything else, until “unassigned” reaches zero.

  4. Not enough for everything? Some envelopes stay empty. That's not a mistake but an honest answer: there's no money for that this month.

How to put money into an envelope — step by step in the article “Budget”.

The chip turned red — “Over-assigned”

You handed out more than you have in your accounts. Reduce what's set aside in one of the envelopes — tap the amount and enter less. The money goes back to “unassigned”.

Two paychecks a month

Money twice a month — you divide it up twice. The first check goes to what's needed until the second. The second goes to the rest and to what comes after it.

Bills before payday — as scheduled payments

Create scheduled payments for rent, utilities, your phone: “Settings” → “Scheduled payments” → “+ Payment”. They appear in the “What’s ahead” block and in the “Calendar” on “Overview”, and a reminder arrives the day before they're due. You see which bills come before payday and which after — and what to cover from the first check. More in the article “Scheduled payments”.

How much you can spend per day

“≈ … a day”

Tap a category on “Budget” — its screen opens. Under “Available” is how much you can spend each day until the end of the month without going negative. With $140 in “Groceries” and ten days left in the month — “≈ $14 a day”. More in the article “Category screen”.

“Running out” on “Overview”

There's still money in the envelope, but you're spending faster than the month is going — the category shows up yellow in the “Running out” block. That's a signal to slow down now, not in a week. More in the article “Overview”.

“Month spending”

A block on “Overview”: whether you're spending more this month than usual by this date.

A red minus

You spent more than the envelope had — the amount is red. Cover it right away, before the month ends.

  1. Tap the red amount on “Budget”.

  2. In the “Overspent” sheet, tap “Cover from another category”. “Where to take the money from” opens: “Unassigned” and every envelope that has money.

  3. Tap “Take …” next to an envelope you can spare. The minus is covered — “Overspending covered!”.

More in the article “Budget”.

The minus lasted to the end of the month

It's reset to zero, but not without a trace: next month's “unassigned” shrinks by the same amount. Your next paycheck ends up smaller by exactly that minus. More in the article “How the budget works”.

Several minuses, and something in “unassigned”

Tap “unassigned” in the “Budget” header → “Cover all overspending”. The app covers all the month's minuses from unassigned money in one go.

A credit card isn't the way out

The envelope is empty, but the credit card has room. The temptation is understandable, but a credit card purchase from an empty envelope is a yellow minus: there's no money, and the debt grows. Next month you'll have to pay it from your new paycheck — which means it'll be smaller again. More in the scenario “Credit cards and debt”.

Record every day

An envelope tells the truth only while all spending is recorded in it. One forgotten purchase from yesterday — and “≈ … a day” is lying to you.

  • The evening reminder: “Settings” → “Planning” → “Reminders” → “Daily spending” → “Remind me to add spending”. It arrives only if you haven't entered anything that day. Right after installing: off.

  • The fastest way to record is at the checkout: by voice or with the receipt scanner. No time to pick a category? — “To inbox”, and sort it out in the evening.

How to get out of the rat race

The goal isn't just to scrape by, but one day to live on money earned a month ago. Go step by step, one at a time.

1

A small cushion

The “Rainy day fund” category with a “Balance” goal — to start, enough for at least a week of spending. Your phone breaks — the money comes from here, not from the food envelope. More in the article “Goals”.

2

Irregular expenses — a little at a time

Insurance, gifts and shoe repairs stop wrecking the month if you set money aside for them every month. A “By date” goal works out how much. More in the scenario “Saving up for something big”.

3

Money a month ahead

Every month, leave a little on top. Once you've saved a whole month, record your paycheck for next month: “Category” → “Income for December”. From then on every month is paid for in advance, and payday no longer matters.

How much you already have for next month — “Month budget” on “Overview”, the “December budget” line. How it's going — “Age of money”: 30 days or more means you're out of the rat race. More in the article “Overview” and in the article “How the budget works”.
