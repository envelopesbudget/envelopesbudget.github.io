# My Budget — Help Center

## What the app can do {#about}

“My Budget” isn't a spending log — it's a full budget built on the envelope method. It can do a lot, and most of it isn't buried deep in menus but hidden in gestures and small touches. This page is a quick tour; after that, each screen gets its own article.

### The essentials {#about-main}

Expense tracking answers the question “where did the money go”. A budget answers a more important one: **“what can it still cover”** — today, until payday, before your vacation.

##### Envelopes

Every dollar gets a job: groceries, rent, vacation. You see how much is left in each envelope, and overspending turns red right away. More in [“How the budget works”](#start).

##### Your data is yours

Your budget lives on your phone and in your cloud. The developer has no access to it. You can lock the cloud with a password — AES-256. More in [“Cloud and backups”](#sync-password).

##### Moving from YNAB

Accounts, categories and your whole history from YNAB 4 or web YNAB move over as a file, and a small glossary shows what familiar terms are called here. More in [“First steps”](#first-steps).

### Quick entry {#about-input}

It's easier to record a purchase at the checkout than to remember it in the evening. That's why there are several ways to do it.

##### To the inbox

No time to pick a category and account at the checkout? Just enter the amount. The “To inbox” button saves it marked “Sort”, and at home the form opens these transactions one by one. Turn it on in “Settings → Data → Inbox”. More in [“Transaction form”](#entry-inbox).

##### By voice

“Whole Foods 70 groceries from account Checking” — and the form is filled in. More in [“Voice input”](#voice).

##### From a receipt

Point the camera at the receipt's QR code — the amount and date fill in by themselves, and from the second time on the app recognizes the store by its register. Works where the app can read your country's receipts. More in [“Receipt scanner”](#receipt).

##### From the home screen

Widgets and shortcuts open the form with one tap, without going into the app. More in [“Widgets and shortcuts”](#widgets).

##### Calculator keypad

Amounts are typed on the app's own keypad with addition and subtraction, split across several categories, transferred between accounts — even in different currencies. More in [“Transaction form”](#entry).

### Plans {#about-plans}

##### Goals

Five kinds: monthly, by a date, keep a balance, percent of income, and a shopping list. The app works out how much to set aside. More in [“Goals”](#goals).

##### Scheduled payments

Rent, subscriptions, loan payments — with a reminder ahead of time and entered in one tap. More in [“Scheduled payments”](#scheduled).

##### Credit cards and debt

Money for paying off a credit card is set aside automatically, and you see the debt month by month. More in the scenario [“Credit cards and debt”](#scenario-credit).

##### Tags and “who owes whom”

A trip, a renovation, shared costs with friends — a tag gathers them from different categories and works out who owes whom. More in [“Payees and tags”](#payees-tags).

### Reports {#about-reports}

Ten reports: where the money went, trend and deviation from usual, money flow from income to spending, income and expenses by month, age of money, net worth, credit card debt. And “Overview” is your home screen: you pick the blocks and arrange them yourself. More in [“Reports”](#reports) and [“Overview”](#overview).

### Devices and cloud {#about-devices}

One budget on your phone, tablet and Windows computer — for two or for the whole family. Sync through Google Drive, Dropbox or WebDAV — Nextcloud, ownCloud, pCloud, your own server; backups are made automatically on a schedule. More in the scenarios [“One budget on several devices”](#scenario-devices) and [“Two or more people, one budget”](#scenario-together).

### Make it yours {#about-yours}

Thirteen languages, sixty currencies, amount and date formats for your country. Seven styles in light and dark, fonts, density, corner shapes, icons. Several budgets: personal, family, work. More in [“Settings”](#settings).

## How the budget works {#start}

The envelope method in ten minutes: where money comes in, how to divide it up, and what happens when the month turns over.

### Envelopes: the big idea {#start-idea}

Imagine you took your paycheck out in cash and split it into envelopes: “Groceries”, “Utilities”, “Vacation”. When you buy groceries, you take money only from that envelope. An empty envelope means there's no more money for that. The app does exactly this, just without the paper.

(1)
##### Money in your accounts

Checking — $1,700

Cash — $200

(2)
##### “Unassigned”

$1,900 — no job yet

(3)
##### Category envelopes

Groceries — $550

Utilities — $170

…and so on, until it reaches 0

(4)
##### Spending

You bought bread — the “Groceries” envelope got thinner

Every dollar in your accounts sits in exactly one place: either in some envelope or in “unassigned”. So at any moment you can see what you can still afford and what you can't. The first goal is to bring “unassigned” down to zero: then **every dollar has a job**. That's not the finish line but the first step — where the method takes you next is [at the end of the article](#start-ladder).

How this differs from the usual “expense tracking” is [below](#start-vs).

### How this differs from expense tracking {#start-vs}

Many people log their spending for years — in a notebook, a spreadsheet, their bank's app — and still can't see why the money runs out before the month does. Expense tracking honestly tells you about the past. Envelopes help you steer the future.

|  | Expense tracking | Envelope budgeting |
|---|---|---|
| When you think about money | After spending: you look at where it went. | Before spending: you decide how much you can spend. |
| The main question | “Where did the money go?” | “What can I still afford?” |
| Overspending | You find out at the end of the month, when it's too late. | You see it right away: the envelope turns red the moment you buy — and you move money over from another one. |
| Money in the bank | One number: “there's $1,400 in checking, so I have money”. | Split up by job: part of it is already promised to rent, part to vacation. Only what's in the right envelope is free. |
| Your paycheck | Just income that will “somehow get spent”. | Money you give a job right away. |
| Irregular expenses | Insurance or a repair hits out of nowhere. | Set aside a little every month — when the bill comes, the money is already there. |

($1,400)
#### Example: “there's still plenty in checking”

It's the middle of the month and there's $1,400 in checking. You want headphones for $280.

*How it works:*
**Expense tracking** sees $1,400 and says: “go ahead”. But rent is due in a week — $800, plus utilities and groceries until payday. The headphones are bought, and rent is short.

**Envelopes** see the same $1,400, but divided up: “Rent” — $800, “Utilities” — $170, “Groceries” — $260, “Fun & hobbies” — $170. There's $170 free for headphones. Want to buy them now? Decide which envelope the missing $110 comes from. You make the decision, and you make it before buying, not after.

(?)
#### So is there expense tracking here?

Yes — and in more detail than a notebook.

*How it works:*
Every purchase is recorded, and “Where the money went”, “Month spending” and the reports show where it all went. It's just not the goal here but a check of the plan: did what you decided match what happened? Next month you'll budget more precisely.

(↻)
#### In short

Expense tracking is the rearview mirror. Envelopes are the steering wheel.

*How it works:*
Looking in the mirror is useful: you see where you came from. But only the wheel helps you move forward — a decision about where the money goes, made in advance.

### Three numbers for every category {#start-envelope}

Turn your phone sideways — the “Budget” screen becomes a table, and the envelope's three numbers are labeled right in the column headers.

[Budget in landscape: columns Category, Planned, Spent, Available; Utilities 100, −92.74, 29.44]
Phone turned sideways: the same “Budget” as a table. In the regular view, “Available” is the amount to the right of the category, and “spent … of …” is the line under the bar.

1. **“Planned”** — how much you put into the envelope this month. It's a decision: “$100 for utilities in November”.

2. **“Spent”** — how much has already left the envelope: all spending in this category this month, with a minus sign. A refund for a returned item reduces this number.

3. **“Available”** — how much you can still spend: carried over from last month + planned − spent.

   *How it works:*
   In the screenshot, “Utilities” shows planned 100, spent 92.74 — so you'd think 7.26 is left. But 29.44 is available: $22.18 went unspent in October and carried over into November. That's how an envelope builds up.

   A green chip means there's money in the envelope, a gray zero means it's empty, and red means a minus: you spent more than there was.

[Budget screen: category groups, amounts on the right, bars and spent lines]

What it looks like on screen and what to tap is in the article [“Budget”](#budget). Here are just the rules the numbers follow.

($)
#### Income goes to “unassigned”, not to a category

Your paycheck doesn't land in an envelope by itself: it goes to “unassigned”, and you divide it up.

*How it works:*
In the transaction form, income is recorded to “Income for November” by default — that is, to this month's “unassigned”. You can also choose “Income for December” — see the section [“Age of money in detail”](#overview) of the “Overview” article.

(↺)
#### A refund goes back to the envelope

Got money back for a returned item? It goes to the category it left from, not to income.

*How it works:*
How to record it — the article [“Transaction form”](#entry), section “Refund for a returned item”.

(⇄)
#### A transfer isn't spending

You withdrew cash, paid off a credit card, moved money to savings — the money is still yours.

*How it works:*
A transfer between budget accounts doesn't touch any envelope. Only a transfer to an account outside the budget (savings, a deposit) leaves the envelopes — then the app asks for a category.

### Month after month {#start-months}

The budget lives in months. What happens to an envelope when the month turns over is one of the key rules.

(+)
#### A plus carries over

Didn't spend it all? The rest moves into the next month and stays in the envelope.

*How it works:*
That's how you save for big and irregular things: put $150 into “Vacation” every month, don't spend it — and by summer there's $900. You can also set money aside for the future right away: you can assign money to any month ahead — the “›” arrow on the “Budget” screen.

(−)
#### A minus resets to zero

Spent more than the envelope had? The category turns red. When the month turns over, the minus is wiped — but not without a trace.

*How it works:*
A minus from your own money (red, wallet icon) reduces next month's “unassigned”: the money is already spent, after all. So it's better to cover a minus right away by moving money from another category: tap the red amount on the “Budget” screen.

A minus on a credit card (yellow, card icon) is a different story: it doesn't eat your money, it grows the card debt. “Unassigned” doesn't take it, but the debt will still have to be paid.

(≡)
#### “Month budget” on “Overview”

The whole month's bookkeeping as one ladder: carried over from last month, income, assigned, and the “unassigned” total.

*How it works:*
If the number on the chip looks odd, look here: you'll see where it came from. More in the article [“Overview”](#overview).

### A credit card in the budget {#start-credit}

The credit card is the least familiar part of the envelope method. There's one rule: a purchase on a credit card spends envelope money just like a purchase on a debit card.

(1)
#### The purchase

You bought groceries on a credit card for $35 — the “Groceries” envelope got $35 thinner, as usual.

(2)
#### Money for the payment sets itself aside

The app moves these $35 into the “Card payments” group, into your credit card's category.

*How it works:*
So by the payment date the money for paying it off is already set apart, and you can't spend it by accident. You don't need to put anything aside yourself.

(3)
#### Paying the card

Pay off the credit card with a transfer from your regular account to the credit card. The money set aside goes to the payment.

*How it works:*
How to record a transfer — the article [“Transaction form”](#entry).

### The method's four rules {#start-rules}

The app doesn't force you to follow them — it suggests them one at a time, when a rule becomes relevant.

(1)
#### Give every dollar a job

Keep assigning money until “unassigned” is zero. Money without a job slips away unnoticed.

(2)
#### Embrace your true expenses

Insurance once a year, a car repair, holiday gifts — these are expenses too. Set aside a little for them every month.

*How it works:*
“By date” goals help: the app works out how much to put in each month to make it in time.

(3)
#### Roll with the punches

Overspending isn't failure. Move money from another envelope and carry on.

*How it works:*
A budget is a plan, not a vow. What matters is that the envelopes honestly show how much is left.

(4)
#### Age your money

The goal is to spend money you earned a month ago, not yesterday.

*How it works:*
“Age of money” on “Overview” keeps track of this. Once it reaches 30 days, you can live on last month's money: details in the article [“Overview”](#overview).

### Zero is only the first step {#start-ladder}

“Unassigned” at zero is a good goal, but not the final one. The method goes further: from “I make it to payday” to “my money works for me”. The steps come in order — skipping them is risky: investing without a cushion means selling your investments the first time the car breaks down.

(1)
#### Every dollar has a job

All the money in your accounts is split into envelopes. You know what you can afford until the end of the month.

**How to tell you're here:** “unassigned” is zero, and no categories are red.

**How the app helps:** the “Unassigned” chip, auto-assign, “Cover all overspending”.

(2)
#### Envelopes for irregular expenses

Insurance, car repairs, gifts and vacations no longer catch you off guard: a little goes toward them every month.

**How to tell you're here:** a big bill arrives, and the money for it is already waiting in its envelope.

**How the app helps:** the goals “By date” and “Monthly” work out how much to put in; the “What’s ahead” block on “Overview” reminds you where money is short.

(3)
#### A month ahead

You spend money you earned a month ago. This month doesn't need the new paycheck — it goes to the next one.

**How to tell you're here:** “Age of money” is 30 days or more.

**How the app helps:** “Age of money” on “Overview”, income “for next month” in the form — details in the article [“Overview”](#overview), section “Age of money in detail”.

(4)
#### An emergency fund

A reserve of three to six months of normal spending. Losing a job or getting sick becomes a setback, not a disaster.

**How to tell you're here:** “Rainy day fund” holds several months' worth of expenses.

**How the app helps:** a “Balance” goal on the “Rainy day fund” category; average monthly spending — in the reports and on the category screen.

(5)
#### Your money works for you

The cushion is built, irregular expenses are covered, you're a month ahead — and now the extra can do more than sit: a deposit, bonds, investments. Money starts making money.

**How to tell you're here:** every month part of your income goes to “Investments”, and you don't touch it for everyday spending.

**How the app helps:** the “Savings” group with an “Investments” category; “Pay yourself” on “Overview” shows what percent of your income you set aside (the norm is 10% or more); a “Tracking” account for a deposit or brokerage account; “Net worth” on “Overview” — how everything you own grows.

How long the road takes depends on your income and habits. The first step is usually the first month. A month ahead takes anywhere from three months to a year. What matters is taking a step every month, not a leap.

### Your first month {#first-steps-month1}

1. **Record every purchase** — right away, at the checkout: “+ Transaction”, the camera for a receipt, or the microphone. Five seconds now instead of an evening with a bank statement later. No time? — “Inbox” (article [“Transaction form”](#entry)).
2. **Money came in — divide it up.** Every paycheck lands in “unassigned”; hand it out to the envelopes on the “Budget” screen.
3. **An envelope turned red — move money** from another one. That's normal: the first month always shows what you underestimated.
4. **Once a week, reconcile your accounts** with the bank — account menu, “Balance reconciliation” (article [“Accounts”](#accounts)).
5. **At the end of the month, look at “Overview”**: “Where the money went”, “Month spending”. Next month you'll assign based on experience.

## One budget on several devices {#scenario-devices}

A phone in your pocket, a tablet at home, a computer at work — and the same budget on all of them. Record a purchase at the checkout, and by evening it's on your laptop. How to set it up and what to keep in mind.

*“Settings”, “Sync” section*

### Who needs this {#scenario-devices-who}

##### Phone and computer

Spending from the phone, at the checkout. Planning the month, reports and reviews on a big screen. For the computer there's the “Windows version”: the same budget, its own look made for a mouse. It syncs through Google Drive — if a computer is involved, choose that cloud on the phone too.

##### Phone and tablet

Dividing a paycheck among envelopes is comfortable on a tablet: “Budget” in landscape is a full-screen table.

##### A new phone

The old one broke or you upgraded — years of budget move over in a couple of minutes. It's the same scenario: the new phone is the second device.

A cloud connects the devices: Google Drive, Dropbox or WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud, pCloud, your own server). Each device sends its edits there and picks up the others'. How it works in detail — in the article [“Cloud and backups”](#sync).

### On the first device {#scenario-devices-first}

The one where you already keep the budget.

1. “Settings”, “Sync” section → “Authorization”.
2. Sign in to the cloud. For Google Drive — with the Google account you'll use on the other devices too. For Dropbox — with your Dropbox username and password, not through Google. Will there be a Windows computer? Choose Google Drive: the Windows version doesn't connect to other clouds.
3. Want to lock the data in the cloud with a password? — “Set a password” in the “Cloud encryption” block. Write the password down: nobody can recover it.
4. Go back to “Overview” and tap the sync icon in the top right. The budget goes to the cloud.

“Authorization” — this is where you connect a cloud. Each cloud in detail — in the article [“Cloud and backups”](sync.md#auth).

### On the second device {#scenario-devices-second}

##### The app was just installed

1. In the first-launch wizard, choose “I already have a budget”.
2. “Load budget from cloud” opens. Connect **the same** cloud with **the same** account.
3. Pick the budget in the list and tap “Load budget”.
4. If a password is set on the first device, set the same one here.
5. Check the currency: “Settings” → “Budget currency”. Details — [below](#scenario-devices-per-device).

##### The app is already installed, with its own budget

“Settings”, “Sync” section → “Load budget from cloud” — then the same steps. Or tap the budget name in the “Overview” header — “Load budget from cloud” is there too.

*How it works:*
If a budget with the same name on this device already has its own data, on the first sync the app asks whether it's one budget or two different ones: “From cloud” or “Merge”. More in the article [“Cloud and backups”](#sync-trouble).

### What's shared and what each device keeps to itself {#scenario-devices-per-device}

What syncs is the **budget data**. Appearance and security settings don't: each device has its own.

| Shared across all devices | Separate on each device |
|---|---|
| Accounts, their icons and colors | Language and formats |
| Categories and groups, category notes | Appearance: style, theme, font, corners |
| Transactions, tags, payees | PIN and fingerprint sign-in |
| Amounts set aside by month, goals and “List” rows | The cloud password — but it must match |
| Scheduled payments | Reminders |
| Photos and PDFs — if “Sync photos” is on | Backup schedule |
| | **Budget currency** |

##### Budget currency — check it on every device

The budget currency symbol lives on the device and doesn't go to the cloud. A loaded budget gets the currency of the budget that was already on this device — usually based on the phone's country. The amounts don't change, only the symbol next to them. Wrong symbol? — “Settings” → “Budget currency”.

##### Receipt photos

Right after installing, photos stay on the device where they were taken. For them to appear everywhere, turn on “Sync photos” on the “Sync” screen — on **every** device.

### Every day {#scenario-devices-daily}

##### You'll rarely need to sync by hand

The app syncs by itself — a few seconds after every time you open it. The number on the sync icon on “Overview” is how many of your edits haven't gone to the cloud yet.

##### Recorded a purchase and putting the phone away

Once the app is minimized, Android doesn't let it keep working, so the edits go up the next time you open it. If you're about to sit down at your computer, tap the sync icon on the phone so the purchase is already there.

##### The sync icon's color

Flashed green — all good. Red — it didn't work; a single failure usually just means no network. If the icon stays red, sync has failed several times in a row — open the “Sync” screen, it says what happened.

### Common mistakes {#scenario-devices-mistakes}

| What happened | Why, and what to do |
|---|---|
| The second device doesn't have the first one's edits | The devices signed in to the cloud with **different** accounts — each writes to its own cloud. Sign in everywhere with one account. |
| “The cloud is password-protected” | The password is set on one device, and on the other it's not set or different. Set **the same** one. |
| Photos arrived but won't open | Same thing: the password differs between devices. |
| Renamed the budget — the second device stopped seeing edits | The budget name is the address of its folder in the cloud. After renaming, rename the budget exactly the same way on the other devices. |
| “Conflict” | The same record was changed on two devices before they synced. The card shows both versions — choose the one you want. |

### Without a cloud {#scenario-devices-no-cloud}

Don't want a cloud? You can move the budget between your own devices directly: as a file or over Wi-Fi on the same network. It's a one-time transfer, not ongoing sync: you'll have to transfer edits again. Good for moving to a new phone. More in the article [“Cloud and backups”](#sync-local).

## Two or more people, one budget {#scenario-together}

A family with shared money: both pay, both record, both see how much is left in each envelope. How to agree with the app and with each other so there's no confusion and no duplicates.

*“Settings”, “Sync” section*

### Who it's for {#scenario-together-who}

The money is shared: paychecks are pooled, and rent, food and vacations come from the common pot. Each person pays with their own card or cash, but the envelopes are shared: both see “Groceries”, and whoever hits the limit first is the one who sees red.

If each of you also has personal money the other shouldn't see, see the scenario [“Couples: shared costs and personal money”](#scenario-couple).

### Setup {#scenario-together-setup}

Technically this is the same budget on several devices — the steps are in the scenario [“One budget on several devices”](#scenario-devices). There's one difference, and it's important.

##### A separate cloud account — just for the budget

All devices must sign in to the cloud with **one** account. But signing in to the husband's personal Google account on the wife's phone is a bad idea: the phone will start syncing his contacts, photos and email.

Create a **new** Google account — just for the budget — and use it to sign in to the app's cloud on every device. Everyone keeps their own personal accounts. Same for Dropbox: a separate Dropbox, signed in with a username and password.

##### One cloud password for everyone

If you set a password, set the **same** one on every phone. The password doesn't sync: it's entered on each device by hand.

### Who paid for what {#scenario-together-whose}

The app doesn't remember which phone a transaction was recorded on. The **account** is what tells spending apart.

##### An account for each person

Give each person their own accounts: “Olivia's card”, “Peter's card”, “Olivia's cash”. Whichever account a purchase is recorded on, that person paid. Each of you reconciles account balances with your own bank — “Balance reconciliation” in the article [“Accounts”](#accounts).

Whose spending this month — “Activity” → “⋮” → “Filter”, filter by account.

##### If you need “who owes whom”

In a shared budget you usually don't: the money is shared anyway. But if you've agreed to split something in half — a tag with “Settle up” turned on for those purchases shows who owes whom how much. More in the article [“Payees and tags”](#payees-tags).

### Assigning money {#scenario-together-assign}

Dividing a paycheck among envelopes is easier for one person — or for two at one screen.

##### Why not each from your own phone

If both of you change how much is set aside in the same category at the same time, whoever's edit reached the cloud last wins — or the app shows a “Conflict” and asks you to choose. The amount in the envelope may not be the one you agreed on.

Agree on who does the assigning. Before you start, open the app and give it a couple of seconds to sync — so you see the other person's latest spending.

##### What both see

“Unassigned”, the balance of each envelope, “Overspent” and “Running out” on “Overview” are shared. If “Groceries” turns yellow, both see it, and you're discussing the same numbers.

### Scheduled payments {#scenario-together-scheduled}

Rent and subscriptions are shared, and the due date arrives on both phones.

##### One person confirms

If two people tap “Confirm” on the same payment before the phones have synced, the transaction is recorded **twice**. Agree on who confirms scheduled payments, or let the app sync before confirming. Delete the extra transaction in “Activity”.

Due date reminders arrive on both phones — for whoever has them turned on. Each person sets them up on their own phone: “Settings” → “Reminders”.

### Every day {#scenario-together-daily}

##### Recorded something? Sync

The app syncs when you open it, not when you minimize it. Recorded a big purchase and know the other person is about to look at the budget? Tap the sync icon on “Overview”.

##### The evening reminder counts you both

“Remind me to add spending” stays quiet if the budget already has spending today — including spending entered by the other person and brought in by sync. If you want it to remind each of you, each should record their own spending the same day.

##### “Recent transactions” are shared

On “Overview” you see what the other person bought: their purchases appear there after a sync.

### Common mistakes {#scenario-together-mistakes}

| What happened | Why, and what to do |
|---|---|
| One phone has more spending than the other | The phones signed in to the cloud with different accounts — that's two different clouds. Sign in everywhere with one, separate account for the budget. |
| A transaction appeared twice | Both of you entered it: for example, a shared receipt was recorded both by the one who paid and by the one who carried the bags. Agree: whoever's card it was records it. |
| A scheduled payment was recorded twice | Both tapped “Confirm”. Delete the extra transaction; from now on one person confirms. |
| “The cloud is password-protected” on one phone | One phone has the password set, and the other has none or a different one. Set the same one. |
| The currency symbol differs between the two of you | The budget currency lives on the device. Fix it in “Settings” → “Budget currency”. |

## "Couples: shared costs and personal money" {#scenario-couple}

Each of you has your own salary and your own spending, while the apartment, food and vacations are split in half. How to handle this in the app today, what it can't do yet, and how to square up once a month.

*the “shared” tag in the transaction form and the “My tags” block on “Overview”*

### What works today {#scenario-couple-today}

What you'd like is this: a shared budget, “Our home”, that both of you keep, plus a personal one each that the other can't see. Through one shared cloud that **won't work** — why is below. But it will work if you pass the shared budget over Wi-Fi at home.

##### Why

A phone signs in to the cloud with one account, and every budget on that phone syncs to that cloud. The cloud password is also one per phone, not per budget. So a shared budget in a shared cloud and a personal one hidden from your partner can't live on the same phone: the personal one would go to the same place as the shared one.

Three approaches work.

##### Personal budgets and a “shared” tag

Each has their own budget — nobody sees the other's money. Shared spending is marked with a tag, and once a month you compare and even out. Details — [below](#scenario-couple-tag).

##### Two budgets, the shared one over Wi-Fi

Each phone has its own budget and the shared “Our home”. Each of you has your own cloud, and you pass the shared budget to each other over Wi-Fi at home. Details — [below](#scenario-couple-wifi).

##### One shared budget for both

All the money in one budget, personal money as its own categories. Both see everything. Good if you have nothing to hide from each other. Details — [below](#scenario-couple-all-shared).

### The “shared” tag approach {#scenario-couple-tag}

Each has their own budget on their own phone: their own cloud, or no cloud at all. Each records shared spending in their own budget as usual and puts a tag on it.

##### How to set it up — each on your own phone

1. Create a category for your share of shared spending — for example, “Family” in the “Fixed expenses” group. Every month, put in as much as you agreed to contribute to the shared pot.
2. Paid for something shared — groceries for home, utilities? Record the purchase in this category.
3. In the “Tags” field, create a “shared” tag and put it on every such purchase.
4. On “Overview”, in the “My tags” block → “⋮” → “Choose tags…”, check “shared”, and set the period to “Total for this month”.

*Why one category and not “Groceries” and “Utilities”:*
Your personal budget answers the question “how much do I spend”. Your share of the shared home is one line item, like rent: “Family”. If you want to see where the shared money goes, open the “shared” tag screen: it has “By category”. In that case, put shared spending in the regular categories, and the tag will gather it together.

### End of the month: who owes whom {#scenario-couple-month}

1. Each of you checks in “My tags” how much you spent on “shared” this month.
2. Add the two amounts and split in half — that's each person's share.
3. Whoever spent less than their share sends the difference to the other.

**Example.** Olivia spent $1,200 on shared costs, Peter spent $800. That's $2,000 in total, so each share is $1,000. Peter sends Olivia $200.

##### How to record the transfer

- **Peter** — a $200 expense in the “Family” category with the “shared” tag: it's also his contribution to the shared pot.
- **Olivia** — $200 coming in from Peter to the same “Family” category, with the same tag: the money returned to the envelope. How to record a refund to a category — in the article [“Transaction form”](#entry).

In Peter's “My tags” it becomes $1,000. In Olivia's it stays $1,200: the block counts only spending. Her $1,000 is on the “shared” tag screen: “Received” $200 and “Net” $1,000.

Compare using “My tags” **before** the transfer — after it, the numbers are already evened out.

*What about “Settle up” on the tag?:*
“Settle up” calculates “spent minus received” in **your** budget — it's good when you split a cost with someone who doesn't write to your budget: a trip with friends, a group gift. For a couple with two budgets it shows only your half of the picture: the other person's contribution isn't in your budget. So here, compare using “My tags”. About “Settle up” — in the article [“Payees and tags”](#payees-tags).

### Two budgets, the shared one over Wi-Fi {#scenario-couple-wifi}

Each phone has two budgets: your own and the shared “Our home”. Each of you has your own cloud — your own account, your own password. Both budgets go to the phone owner's cloud, and the other person doesn't see them there. You pass only “Our home” to each other — over Wi-Fi, when you're both at home.

##### How to set it up

1. Each of you connects a cloud on your own phone with your own account, as usual.
2. Olivia creates the “Our home” budget: tap the budget name in the “Overview” header — there's the list of budgets and a new budget. Peter creates an empty budget with the same name on his phone.
3. The first time, Peter receives “Our home” from Olivia over Wi-Fi (how — below) and chooses “Replace completely”: his empty budget becomes a copy of Olivia's.

##### Exchanging at home

1. **Both of you open “Our home”.** The exchange happens between the open budgets.
2. “Settings” → “Local transfer”. Olivia: “Start the server”. Peter: “Scan the QR” → “Receive the data” → “Merge”.
3. Olivia: “Stop the server”.

The exchange goes both ways: Peter gets Olivia's edits, and his edits go to Olivia — transactions and receipt photos alike. It doesn't matter which of you sends and which receives.

**“This looks like a different budget” — tap “Cancel”.** If one of you has your own budget open instead of “Our home”, the two budgets share no accounts or transactions, and the app asks before merging. “Continue anyway” would pour personal spending into the shared budget, and there'd be no way to separate it again.

*What if the budgets did merge?:*
You can't separate them by hand, but you can roll both back to an automatic backup — the app makes these itself, every 5 days by default. The exchange goes both ways, so two budgets are spoiled: Peter's personal one (“Our home” came into it), and Olivia's “Our home” (Peter's personal spending came into it). Each fixes their own on their own phone.

1. Open the spoiled budget. “Settings” → “Backups”.
2. Find a backup of **this** budget (its name is in the backup's row) dated **before** the exchange. Tap the clock-with-arrow icon → “Restore”. After that, **don't tap “Sync now”**: the merged budget is still in the cloud.
3. Delete this budget's sync file in your cloud — as in the article [“Cloud and backups”](#sync-rollback), steps 2–4. Each of you has your own cloud, and only your phone writes this budget there, so the part about several devices doesn't apply.
4. Edits made after the backup will be lost. Shared ones can be recovered: once Olivia has rolled back “Our home”, exchange once more with both of you on “Our home”, and the shared edits return to her from Peter's “Our home” — his copy is clean. Personal spending entered after the backup will have to be recorded again.

Before the first exchange it's worth making backups by hand — each on your own phone, and of **both** budgets: a backup is made for the open budget. Open your own budget → “Backups” → “Create a backup now”, then open “Our home” and repeat. That way you'd roll back not to a five-day-old automatic backup but to the state right before the exchange.

*Does the cloud password have to match?:*
No. The Wi-Fi transfer is encrypted with the confirmation code from the sending phone's screen; the backup password plays no part in it. The password in the “Cloud encryption” block locks only **your** cloud. It has to be the same only when two phones write to one cloud — here the clouds are different.

*What if you edited away from home?:*
Each of you keeps “Our home” on your own phone: record a purchase in the store, and it's immediately in your copy and your cloud. Exchange at home — and both have everything. If one phone still has an older version of the app, the receiver's edits won't go back — the app says so; then exchange again, swapping roles. If you both edited the same transaction, the later edit stays, as with regular sync.

The trade-off: the shared budget is in sync only once you're both home and have exchanged.

### One shared budget for both {#scenario-couple-all-shared}

If there are no personal secrets, it's simplest to keep everything together — then you see the whole picture and a single “unassigned” for both of you.

- Setup — as in the scenario [“Two or more people, one budget”](#scenario-together): a separate cloud account for the budget, signed in on both phones.
- Personal money — as categories of its own: “Olivia's personal”, “Peter's personal”. Each month the agreed amount goes into them; how to spend it is up to the owner.
- Accounts — each has their own: “Olivia's card”, “Peter's card”. That way you see who paid.

The trade-off: both of you see everything — spending from the “personal” categories and card balances alike.

### What's not there yet {#scenario-couple-not-yet}

A shared budget that syncs through the cloud for both of you on its own, alongside personal ones hidden from your partner, and moving a shared expense from a personal budget to the shared one in one tap — not there yet. We'll build this when people need it. If that's you, write to “Telegram chat” (“Settings”, “Help & contact” section): the more such messages, the sooner.

## Freelance and irregular income {#scenario-freelance}

Money comes in when the client pays: feast one month, famine the next. How to stop living from one payment to the next with an income like that, set money aside for taxes, and know what you really earn.

*“Budget”, “Overview”, “Reports” — everything below*

### The big idea {#scenario-freelance-idea}

With a salary, the month is predictable: paid on the 5th, enough until the next 5th. A freelancer doesn't know the month's income in advance — so you can't plan from it. Plan from the money you **already have**.

##### Spend what you earned earlier

The month lives on what came in before. What comes in now works for the next month. [How](#scenario-freelance-last-month).

##### Smooth it out with a cushion

A good month tops up the reserve, a lean month draws on it. [How](#scenario-freelance-buffer).

##### Set the tax aside right away

A percentage of each payment goes into its own envelope before the money gets spent. [How](#scenario-freelance-taxes).

### Spend last month's money {#scenario-freelance-last-month}

Record each payment not to this month but to the **next** one. Then by the 1st, the whole month is already paid for with money you've earned, and you divide it among the envelopes once — not in pieces after every payment.

##### How to record a payment

1. “+ Transaction” → “+ Incoming”, payee — the client, amount.
2. Tap the “Category” field — “Where to put the income” opens.
3. Choose “Income for December” instead of “Income for November”. Save.

The transaction date stays the real one. Only the budget month the money shows up in changes.

##### Where to start

For the first month you'll have to both spend what you have and save for the next one. Start with a payment you haven't assigned yet. Until you have enough for a month ahead, put at least part of it into next month: the app shows how much is already there in “Month budget” on “Overview” — the “December budget” line.

The goal is an “Age of money” of 30 days or more: it means you're spending last month's money. More in the article [“Overview”](#overview-age-month).

### A cushion for lean months {#scenario-freelance-buffer}

Even with money a month ahead, two lean months in a row happen. A cushion is an envelope that gets you through them.

##### How to set it up

1. The “Rainy day fund” category — or one of your own, “Cushion”.
2. A “Balance” goal: the amount is your usual spending for 3–6 months. How much that is — “Average income per month” and “Spending trend”, below.
3. In a good month, put everything above the usual into the cushion.

The “Balance” goal makes sure the reserve doesn't melt away: spend part of it, and the app asks you to put it back. More in the article [“Goals”](#goals-balance).

##### A lean month

There wasn't enough for the envelopes, and spending went into the red. Tap the red amount on “Budget” → “Cover from another category” → on the “Rainy day fund” row, “Take …”. The money moves from the cushion to where the minus is. More in the article [“Budget”](#budget-screen).

### Taxes — straight into their own envelope {#scenario-freelance-taxes}

The tax on a payment is easy to spend: the money is on the card, and the tax is due next quarter.

##### A “% income” goal

1. The “Taxes” category.
2. A “% income” goal — the percentage you pay. Under the percentage, leave “From this month's income”: tax is calculated on the money that came in, not on the average.
3. Each payment updates the goal by itself: more came in, the goal went up, and “… to go” tells you how much to add.

What you set aside builds up in the envelope until the payment date: a plus carries over from month to month. More in the article [“Goals”](#goals-percent).

*If you record income to next month:*
Then this month's “% income” goal is known on the 1st: the month's income arrived last month. Handy — the tax is set aside first thing, along with the other envelopes.

### Money came in — divide it up in one tap {#scenario-freelance-income}

Dividing up each payment by hand gets tiring. The “By percentage” auto-assign does it for you.

##### Set it up once

1. On “Budget”, tap “unassigned” in the header — “Auto-assign the budget” opens.
2. “By percentage” → “Set percentages”: for example, “Taxes” — 5%, “Rainy day fund” — 20%, “Investments” — 10%.
3. The percentages are remembered. Whatever isn't covered by a percentage stays in “unassigned” — assign it to the month's envelopes yourself.

From then on: money came in — tap “unassigned” → “By percentage”. The app divides up exactly what's unassigned right now.

*Why percentages and not amounts:*
With an irregular income, a fixed amount is impossible one month and laughable the next. A percentage rises and falls along with your income. Rounding — the “To whole”, “To 10”, “To 100” chips on the same sheet.

### Clients and projects {#scenario-freelance-clients}

##### The client is the payee

Record every payment with the client in the “Payee” field. Tap the client in “Settings” → “Payees” — their screen opens: “Income trends” shows how much they pay on average, and the “Year” period shows all their payments for the year. You can see who feeds you and who pays twice a year. More in the article [“Payees and tags”](#payees-tags-payee).

##### The project is a tag

Create a tag for the project: “cafe website”. Put it on both the client's payment and the project's costs: a service subscription, a taxi to a meeting, a font. The tag screen shows “Spent”, “Received” and “Net” — what the project really brought in. More in the article [“Payees and tags”](#payees-tags).

### Expected payments {#scenario-freelance-expected}

The invoice is out, and the money was promised by the 15th. To not forget and to see what's coming, create a scheduled payment.

1. “Settings” → “Scheduled payments” → “+ Payment”.
2. “+ Incoming”, the client, the amount, the date payment was promised, “Repeat” — “Once”.
3. The payment appears in “What’s ahead” and in the “Calendar” on “Overview”.
4. The money arrived on time — “Confirm” on the confirmation screen. Earlier than due — swipe the payment row left — “Enter”. The client is late — open the payment and change the “Date” field.

Until the payment is confirmed, it isn't in the budget: envelopes don't get filled with promised money. More in the article [“Scheduled payments”](#scheduled).

### What to watch {#scenario-freelance-reports}

| Where | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| “Overview” → “Month budget” | “Average income per month” over 12 months and “December budget” — how much you already have for next month. |
| “Overview” → “Age of money” | 30 days or more — you're living on last month's money. |
| “Overview” → “Month spending” | Whether you're spending more than usual right now. |
| “Reports” → “Income and expenses” | A month-by-month table: income by source — each client gets its own row — expenses by group, and “Savings rate”. You see both seasonality and who pays how much. |
| “Reports” → “Spending trend” | Your usual monthly spending — the basis for sizing the cushion. |
| “Reports” → “Money flow” | Where the money you earned went over a period — and how much is left. |
| “Reports” → “Age of money” | How your time buffer grows month by month. |

More about the reports in the article [“Reports”](#reports).

### Keep work money separate {#scenario-freelance-business}

If your work has its own account and its own expenses — services, equipment, an accountant — you can keep them in a **second budget**, for example “Work”. Tap the budget name in the “Overview” header — there's the budget switcher and a new budget.

The work budget holds payments and work expenses. What you take for living goes as a transfer to your personal card: in the work budget it's spending, in the personal one it's income. That way your personal budget lives on an even amount every month, and all the ups and downs stay in the work budget.

## Paycheck to paycheck {#scenario-paycheck}

The money runs out before the month does, and payday is still a week away. How to divide up what you have, make it to the next paycheck without red, and step by step get out of the rat race.

*“Budget”, “Overview” and the category screen — everything below*

### The big idea {#scenario-paycheck-idea}

Envelopes don't give you more money than you have. But they show you **in advance** whether it'll last until payday — not on the last day, but on the first.

##### Assign only what you have

An envelope is filled with money that's already in your accounts. A promised paycheck isn't money yet. [How](#scenario-paycheck-now).

##### Essentials first

Housing, food, getting to work, medicine, a loan payment. Everything else comes after. [How](#scenario-paycheck-now).

##### A minus isn't failure

Move money from another envelope and carry on. [How](#scenario-paycheck-minus).

### Payday — divide it up right away {#scenario-paycheck-now}

Until money is divided up, it feels like a lot. Divided into envelopes, it tells the truth: $200 for food until the 5th, and that's it.

##### In order

1. Open “Budget”. At the top is “unassigned”: that much money still has no job.
2. First fill the envelopes you can't get to the next paycheck without: rent, utilities, “Groceries”, getting to work, medicine, the minimum loan payment.
3. Then everything else, until “unassigned” reaches zero.
4. Not enough for everything? Some envelopes stay empty. That's not a mistake but an honest answer: there's no money for that this month.

How to put money into an envelope — step by step in the article [“Budget”](#budget-screen).

*The chip turned red — “Over-assigned”:*
You handed out more than you have in your accounts. Reduce what's set aside in one of the envelopes — tap the amount and enter less. The money goes back to “unassigned”.

### Two paychecks a month {#scenario-paycheck-two}

Money twice a month — you divide it up twice. The first check goes to what's needed until the second. The second goes to the rest and to what comes after it.

##### Bills before payday — as scheduled payments

Create scheduled payments for rent, utilities, your phone: “Settings” → “Scheduled payments” → “+ Payment”. They appear in the “What’s ahead” block and in the “Calendar” on “Overview”, and a reminder arrives the day before they're due. You see which bills come before payday and which after — and what to cover from the first check. More in the article [“Scheduled payments”](#scheduled).

### How much you can spend per day {#scenario-paycheck-until}

##### “≈ … a day”

Tap a category on “Budget” — its screen opens. Under “Available” is how much you can spend each day until the end of the month without going negative. With $140 in “Groceries” and ten days left in the month — “≈ $14 a day”. More in the article [“Category screen”](#category-screen).

##### “Running out” on “Overview”

There's still money in the envelope, but you're spending faster than the month is going — the category shows up yellow in the “Running out” block. That's a signal to slow down now, not in a week. More in the article [“Overview”](#overview-blocks).

##### “Month spending”

A block on “Overview”: whether you're spending more this month than usual by this date.

### A red minus {#scenario-paycheck-minus}

You spent more than the envelope had — the amount is red. Cover it right away, before the month ends.

1. Tap the red amount on “Budget”.
2. In the “Overspent” sheet, tap “Cover from another category”. “Where to take the money from” opens: “Unassigned” and every envelope that has money.
3. Tap “Take …” next to an envelope you can spare. The minus is covered — “Overspending covered!”.

More in the article [“Budget”](#budget-screen).

*The minus lasted to the end of the month:*
It's reset to zero, but not without a trace: next month's “unassigned” shrinks by the same amount. Your next paycheck ends up smaller by exactly that minus. More in the article [“How the budget works”](#start-months).

*Several minuses, and something in “unassigned”:*
Tap “unassigned” in the “Budget” header → “Cover all overspending”. The app covers all the month's minuses from unassigned money in one go.

### A credit card isn't the way out {#scenario-paycheck-card}

The envelope is empty, but the credit card has room. The temptation is understandable, but a credit card purchase from an empty envelope is a **yellow** minus: there's no money, and the debt grows. Next month you'll have to pay it from your new paycheck — which means it'll be smaller again. More in the scenario [“Credit cards and debt”](#scenario-credit-yellow).

### Record every day {#scenario-paycheck-daily}

An envelope tells the truth only while all spending is recorded in it. One forgotten purchase from yesterday — and “≈ … a day” is lying to you.

- The evening reminder: “Settings” → “Planning” → “Reminders” → “Daily spending” → “Remind me to add spending”. It arrives only if you haven't entered anything that day. **Right after installing:** off.
- The fastest way to record is at the checkout: [by voice](#voice) or [with the receipt scanner](#receipt). No time to pick a category? — [“To inbox”](#entry-inbox), and sort it out in the evening.

### How to get out of the rat race {#scenario-paycheck-out}

The goal isn't just to scrape by, but one day to live on money earned a month ago. Go step by step, one at a time.

(1)
##### A small cushion

The “Rainy day fund” category with a “Balance” goal — to start, enough for at least a week of spending. Your phone breaks — the money comes from here, not from the food envelope. More in the article [“Goals”](#goals-balance).

(2)
##### Irregular expenses — a little at a time

Insurance, gifts and shoe repairs stop wrecking the month if you set money aside for them every month. A “By date” goal works out how much. More in the scenario [“Saving up for something big”](#scenario-saving).

(3)
##### Money a month ahead

Every month, leave a little on top. Once you've saved a whole month, record your paycheck for next month: “Category” → “Income for December”. From then on every month is paid for in advance, and payday no longer matters.

How much you already have for next month — “Month budget” on “Overview”, the “December budget” line. How it's going — “Age of money”: 30 days or more means you're out of the rat race. More in the article [“Overview”](#overview-age-month) and in the article [“How the budget works”](#start-ladder).

## Credit cards and debt {#scenario-credit}

A credit card, a car loan, a debt you had before the app. How to set them up, how to pay so the budget doesn't lie, and how to watch the debt shrink.

*“Accounts”, “Budget” → “Card payments”, “Reports” → “Card debt” and “Net worth”*

### Credit card {#scenario-credit-card}

There's one rule: a purchase on a credit card spends envelope money just like a purchase on a debit card. The difference is where that money waits: not in an account but in the credit card's category, until you pay the bank.

##### How to set it up

1. “Accounts” → “⋮” → “Add an account”.
2. Type — “Credit card”. Name — whatever you call the card.
3. Amount — how much you owe the bank right now. Type it and tap “±” to make it negative.
4. Save. The card goes to the “Credit cards” section, and on “Budget” a category with the same name appears in the “Card payments” group.

Leave that category alone: the app uses it to count the debt of this particular card. More in the article [“Accounts”](#accounts-screen).

##### A purchase

Record it as usual: account — the credit card, category — what you bought. You bought groceries for $35 — “Groceries” got $35 thinner, and the same $35 moved into the credit card's category for the payment. You don't need to set anything aside yourself.

### Debt you had before you started {#scenario-credit-old-debt}

The starting debt — $800 on the card from last year — isn't set aside by the app: there was never money for it in the envelopes.

Set money aside for it in the credit card's category, like any envelope: on “Budget”, tap its amount and enter what you can manage this month. All at once or a little at a time — your call. Until you set it aside, there's no money for paying it off.

### The yellow minus {#scenario-credit-yellow}

You spent more on the credit card than the envelope had — the amount turned **yellow**, with a card icon. Red, with a wallet icon, is a minus from your own money.

##### Red eats your money

A minus on a debit card or cash. At the end of the month it reduces next month's “unassigned”.

##### Yellow grows your debt

You didn't spend money — you borrowed it. Only the covered part went into the credit card's category; for the rest, there's no money for the payment.

##### What to do

Cover a yellow minus like a red one: tap the amount → “Cover from another category” → “Take …” from another envelope. Then the money for the payment is topped up automatically. Couldn't do it? Next month, put the difference straight into the credit card's category.

### Paying the card {#scenario-credit-payment}

How much to pay the bank is shown in the credit card's category on “Budget”: that much is already set aside for the payment.

1. “+ Transaction”, account — your debit card.
2. “Payee” → the “Transfers” section → your credit card. The form header turns gray, and no category is needed.
3. Amount — how much you're paying. Save.

The money on your debit card goes down, so does the credit card debt, and with it the amount set aside in the credit card's category. The envelopes don't change: you did the spending earlier, when you made the purchases. More in the article [“Transaction form”](#entry-transfer).

*Pay it all or the minimum payment:*
The credit card's category holds exactly as much as you spent on the card from your envelopes. Pay everything set aside each month, and no new debt builds up. Pay only the minimum, and the rest stays in the category, waiting; the bank charges interest on the remaining debt.

### Card interest and fees {#scenario-credit-card-interest}

The bank charged interest or a service fee — that's spending.

Record an expense: account — the credit card, payee — the bank, category — one of its own, for example “Interest and fees”. The envelope shrinks, and the money for the payment is set aside, as with any purchase. It shows in the reports too: what this credit costs you.

### Loan {#scenario-credit-loan}

A car loan, a mortgage, an installment plan — money you've already spent and are paying back in parts.

##### How to set it up

1. “Accounts” → “⋮” → “Add an account”.
2. Type — “Loan”. Amount — how much is left to repay according to the bank statement, with a minus sign (“±”).
3. The account goes to the “Debts” section. It's off budget: this money has no envelopes, and the budget doesn't touch the starting debt.

##### An envelope for the payment

Create a category for the monthly payment — for example, “Loans” in the “Fixed expenses” group — and a “Monthly” goal for the payment amount. Every month the goal reminds you to set the money aside. More in the article [“Goals”](#goals-monthly).

### Paying a loan {#scenario-credit-loan-payment}

1. “+ Transaction”, account — the card you pay from.
2. “Payee” → “Transfers” → the loan account.
3. The loan is off budget, so the app asks for a category: choose “Loans”. The money leaves the envelope, and the debt on the loan account goes down.

*The bank says $80 of the payment is interest:*
Record the whole payment as a single transfer, as above. Then once a month, record the interest on the loan account itself: “Outgoing”, account — the loan, payee — the bank, amount — the interest. No category needed: the account is off budget. Then the debt in the app matches the bank, and the “Loans” envelope gives up exactly your payment.

*A payment reminder:*
Create a scheduled payment as a transfer: “Settings” → “Scheduled payments” → “+ Payment”, “Payee” → “Transfers” → the loan account, category “Loans”, “Repeat” — “Monthly”. The payment appears on both accounts' screens, a reminder arrives in advance, and “Confirm” records the transfer. The same goes for a credit card payment: from your debit card to the credit card. More in the article [“Scheduled payments”](#scheduled-new).

### Watching the debt {#scenario-credit-watch}

| Where | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| “Accounts” | The “Credit cards” and “Debts” sections — how much you owe right now. |
| “Budget” → “Card payments” | How much is set aside to pay off each card. |
| “Reports” → “Card debt” | The debt on all credit cards month by month: is it shrinking or growing. Available if you have a credit card. |
| “Reports” → “Net worth” | Everything you own minus all debts. Every loan payment moves the line up. |

More about the reports in the article [“Reports”](#reports-capital).

## Saving up for something big {#scenario-saving}

A laptop, a renovation, a car, a down payment on an apartment. How to set aside a little at a time, see whether you'll make it by the deadline, and not spend the savings by accident.

*“Budget”, “Goals”, the category screen*

### An envelope just for the purchase {#scenario-saving-envelope}

Money for a laptop mixed in with money for living gets spent first. A separate envelope protects it: while it sits in “Laptop”, it doesn't show up in “Groceries”.

1. On “Budget”, find the group that holds “Big purchases” and “Travel & vacation”.
2. Tap “⊕” next to its name — a new category. Name it after the purchase: “Laptop”.
3. Or save in the ready-made “Big purchases” if there's only one purchase.

More in the article [“Budget”](#budget-screen).

### A “By date” goal {#scenario-saving-goal}

Tell the app how much and by which month — it works out how much to put in each month.

1. Tap the category on “Budget” → the “Goal” card → “Add a goal”.
2. “Goal type” — “By date”.
3. “Amount” — the price of the purchase, “Save up by month” — when you need it.
4. “Save”.

A $2,400 laptop in six months — the goal will ask for $400 a month. Miss a month, and the shortfall is spread over the remaining ones, so the payment grows. Put in more, and the next ones shrink. More in the article [“Goals”](#goals-bydate).

*Saving without a deadline:*
A “Balance” goal: the envelope should hold this amount, and when doesn't matter. It suits “a new car someday” and an emergency cushion. More in the article [“Goals”](#goals-balance).

### Am I on track {#scenario-saving-pace}

##### A quick estimate on the category screen

Tap the category — a “By date” goal has a switch under the bar: “By amount” and “By date”. Move “Save per month” — you see which month you'll get there. Move the month — you see how much to put in. Like the new date? — “Make it the goal date: …”. More in the article [“Goals”](#goals-card).

##### Short this month

On “Budget”, under the amount there's a flag and “… to go”. The “Underfunded” chip above the list leaves only those categories. On “Overview”, the shortfall shows in the “Goals” and “What’s ahead” blocks.

### Where the money is {#scenario-saving-where}

Savings are often kept in a separate account: a savings account, a deposit, a piggy bank. How you set up that account decides whether the money stays in the envelope.

##### On budget — for a purchase

An account of the “Bank account” type. You moved $400 from checking to savings — the “Laptop” envelope didn't change: the money moved, but it's still yours and still in the budget. The goal sees it and doesn't ask again. **Saving for a purchase? Choose this option.**

##### Off budget — for wealth

An account of the “Tracking” type: investments, a retirement deposit. The app records a transfer there as money leaving an envelope — it asks for a category, and the envelope empties. This won't work for a purchase: the goal will ask for money again, even though you have it.

More in the article [“Accounts”](#accounts-kinds).

### Several goals at once {#scenario-saving-many}

A vacation, a laptop and holiday gifts — there may not be enough money for everything this month.

“Budget” → “⋮” → “Assign to goals”. The app spreads “unassigned” across all goals at once. Not enough for everything? Goals get money in order, top to bottom, as the categories are arranged on “Budget”. Keep what matters higher up: “⋮” → “Change the order”. More in the article [“Goals”](#goals-allocate).

### The purchase {#scenario-saving-buy}

1. Record the purchase as usual — in the “Laptop” category. The envelope empties — that's what you were saving for.
2. It came out cheaper? The rest stays in the envelope. Need it elsewhere? On “Budget”, tap the “Laptop” amount and enter less: the difference goes back to “unassigned”.
3. It came out more expensive? The amount turns red. Tap it → “Take …” from another envelope.
4. Don't need the goal anymore? “Budget” → “⋮” → “Goals” → tap the category → “Delete”. The money in the envelope stays. The category with the purchase itself can't be deleted — only hidden.

*Bought it with a credit card:*
That works too: the money from “Laptop” is set aside for paying off the card. Bought it on credit or in installments? That's a debt; how to track it is in the scenario [“Credit cards and debt”](#scenario-credit-loan).

### How much I save {#scenario-saving-pay-yourself}

The “Pay yourself” block on “Overview” shows what percent of your income you set aside — for the month, three months and the year. The norm is 10%. Choose which categories count as “saved” in “Settings → Budget → Pay yourself”: “Big purchases”, “Laptop”, “Rainy day fund”. More in the article [“Overview”](#overview-blocks).

## A trip with friends {#scenario-trip}

You paid for lodging for four, a friend paid for dinner, gas is split in half, and abroad there's cash in another currency on top of that. How to save for a trip, see what it cost, and not lose track of who owes whom.

*the trip's tag, “Settle up” on the tag screen, the transaction form*

### Before the trip {#scenario-trip-before}

Save in “Travel & vacation” with a “By date” goal: the amount is what you expect to spend, the month is when you're going. The app works out the monthly contribution. More in the scenario [“Saving up for something big”](#scenario-saving-goal).

### A tag for the trip {#scenario-trip-tag}

Trip spending lands in different categories — “Groceries”, “Transport”, “Travel & vacation”. A tag gathers it together.

1. On the very first purchase of the trip, open “Additional” → “Tags” → type a name, for example “Tahoe”, → “Create tag “…””.
2. Put this tag on every trip purchase.
3. The tag's “⋮” → “Show in My tags” — the trip total will be on “Overview”.

The tag screen shows “Spent” and “By category” — what the trip cost and where the money went. More in the article [“Payees and tags”](#payees-tags-tag).

### You pay for everyone {#scenario-trip-pay-for-all}

You paid for lodging for four: $800. Your share is $200, and the other $600 should come back to you.

##### Split the amount into your share and your friends' share

1. Record the $800 purchase, account — your card, tag — “Tahoe”.
2. “Category” → “Split the amount”.
3. The first part is “Travel & vacation”, $200: that's your share.
4. The second part is also “Travel & vacation”, $600, and on it “+ tag” → a new tag “Tahoe: friends owe”.
5. Open the “Tahoe: friends owe” tag screen → “⋮” → “Settle up”.

Now the tag screen says “to collect $600”: that's how much you're owed. More about split amounts in the article [“Transaction form”](#entry-split).

*Why a second tag and not just one:*
“Settle up” calculates spent minus received. If you turned it on for “Tahoe”, your own share would also hang there as “To collect”, and the settlement would never balance. A tag on just your friends' share comes to zero once everything is paid back.

### A friend paid you back {#scenario-trip-paid-back}

1. “+ Transaction” → “Incoming”. Payee — the friend, amount — how much they paid back, account — where the money arrived.
2. “Category” → “To an expense category…” → “Travel & vacation”. This isn't income — it's your money returning to the envelope.
3. Tags — “Tahoe” and “Tahoe: friends owe”.

Once all three have paid you back, the “Tahoe: friends owe” tag screen says “Settled” in green. And for “Tahoe”, “Net” will be $600 less than “Spent” — for lodging, exactly your share remains. More in the article [“Transaction form”](#entry-refund).

### A friend paid {#scenario-trip-friend-paid}

A friend paid for dinner, and your share is $50. No money has left your card yet — there's nothing to record.

When you pay the friend back, record it then: an expense, payee — the friend, category — “Travel & vacation”, tag — “Tahoe”. Until you pay, keep the debt in your head or in a note — the app doesn't track other people's wallets.

### Abroad {#scenario-trip-abroad}

##### Paying with your own card

The bank charges the money in your card's currency. Record the amount the bank charged — you can see it in the bank's app. The amount in local currency, if you need it, goes in the note: “€42 dinner”.

##### You took cash in a foreign currency

Create a “Cash €” account: “Accounts” → “⋮” → “Add an account”, type “Tracking”, “Account currency” — €. Only an off-budget account can have its own currency. How to work with it is below.

##### Exchanging and spending cash

1. You exchanged money — record a transfer from your card to “Cash €”. Under the payee, “Credited” and “Rate” appear: how many euros you got.
2. The account is off budget, so the app asks for a category: “Travel & vacation”. The money left the envelope right at the exchange.
3. Record cash spending in euros on the “Cash €” account — no category needed, the envelope has already paid. Add the “Tahoe” tag as usual.
4. Euros left over and exchanged back? A transfer from “Cash €” to your card, “Category” → “To an expense category…” → “Travel & vacation”. The money returns to the envelope.

More about currencies in the scenario [“Several currencies and the exotic”](#scenario-currencies).

### After the trip {#scenario-trip-after}

- The “Tahoe” tag screen: “Net” is what the trip cost you after all paybacks, “By category” shows where the money went. Handy when you save for the next one. Spending in euros is on a separate line: there's no conversion at a rate, and the exchange itself is a transfer, so it isn't in “Spent”.
- Don't need the tag in the picker anymore? The tag's “⋮” → “Hide from picker”. It stays on the transactions.
- Money left in “Travel & vacation”? Let it sit until the next vacation: a plus carries over from month to month.

## Several currencies and the exotic {#scenario-currencies}

Your salary in one currency, savings in another, a little bitcoin and trips abroad. What the app can do with currencies, where the limits are, and how to keep your books so the envelopes don't lie.

*“Settings” → “Budget currency”, off-budget accounts, a transfer with a rate*

### The main rule {#scenario-currencies-rule}

**A budget has one currency.** All envelopes, all budget accounts and “unassigned” are in it. Other currencies live in accounts **off budget**: you see how much is there, but that money isn't divided among the envelopes.

*Why it works this way:*
An envelope answers “how much can I spend on food”. If it holds both dollars and euros, the answer changes every day along with the exchange rate. One currency in the envelopes — and the answer is always the same.

### Budget currency {#scenario-currencies-budget-currency}

“Settings” → “Budget” → “Budget currency”. The list has sixty currencies, the current one in the first row, with a search by name and symbol at the top: “zloty” finds “Złoty”.

Only the symbol changes — the app doesn't convert amounts. If you kept your budget in dollars and pick euros, $1,000 becomes €1,000. That's why you choose the currency at the start, not in the middle of your bookkeeping.

*A budget for each currency:*
Each budget has its own currency. Living between two countries? You can keep two budgets: “Home” in dollars and “Berlin” in euros. Tap the budget name in the “Overview” header — there's the budget switcher and a new budget; you'll be asked for the currency when creating it.

### An account in another currency {#scenario-currencies-foreign-account}

1. “Accounts” → “⋮” → “Add an account”.
2. Type — “Tracking” (or “Loan” if it's a debt). Only an off-budget account can have its own currency.
3. In the “Account currency” section, tap the “Currency” field — a list of sixty currencies opens, the same as for the budget: the current one in the first row, a search by name and symbol at the top. Names are in the country's own language: “zloty” finds “Złoty”.
4. Cryptocurrency — chips under the field: ₿, USDT, USDC.
5. Not there? Type it yourself in “Or type your own (ticker)”: ETH, a stock or fund ticker. The account's amounts will be labeled with it.
6. Amount — how much is in the account in that currency.

The account goes to the “Off budget” section, with amounts in its own symbol: “€1,200.00”. More in the article [“Accounts”](#accounts-kinds).

### Exchange {#scenario-currencies-exchange}

Bought euros with dollars? That's a transfer between your own accounts, just with a rate.

1. “+ Transaction”, account — your dollar card, “Payee” → “Transfers” → the euro account.
2. Amount — how many dollars left.
3. Under the payee are “Credited” and “Rate”. Enter either the rate or how many euros arrived — the other is calculated. The button next to the rate flips its direction: dollars per one euro or euros per one dollar.
4. The euro account is off budget — the app asks for a category: which envelope the money left. For example, “Rainy day fund” or “Investments”.

More in the article [“Transaction form”](#entry-transfer).

*Sold the euros back:*
A transfer from the euro account to your card. The “Category” field opens “Where to put the income”: “Income for …” — if it's new money to hand out, “To an expense category…” — if it's returning to the envelope it left.

### Living in one country, saving in another {#scenario-currencies-two-countries}

The budget is in the currency you're paid in and spend every day: dollars, euros, pounds. Money back home, a deposit in another currency, dollars in a “rainy day” stash — off-budget accounts, each in its own currency. The envelopes count what you live on, and the rest is visible next to them on “Accounts”.

Record card spending abroad as the amount the bank charged in your card's currency. The amount in local currency, if you need it, goes in the note. More in the scenario [“A trip with friends”](#scenario-trip-abroad).

### Cryptocurrency {#scenario-currencies-crypto}

An off-budget account, the ₿, USDT or USDC chip under the “Currency” field — and the “Cryptocurrency” switch turns on by itself: “8 decimal places”. For your own ticker (ETH, SOL), turn it on by hand. Picked a regular currency from the list? The switch turns off.

The number of decimal places is chosen once: once the account has transactions, the switch says “Cannot change: the account has transactions”.

### Currencies without cents {#scenario-currencies-no-cents}

The yen, won and dong have no minor units, but the app still shows “.00”. It's only the display: type whole numbers as usual. With “With a decimal point” entry, 1500 is 1,500, not 15.00. How amounts are typed — “Settings” → “Language and formats” → “Amount entry”.

### How amounts are written {#scenario-currencies-format}

“Settings” → “Language and formats” → “Amount format”: the thousands and decimal separators, the currency symbol on the left or right. Out of the box it follows the interface language: in English, “$1,234.56”. English on a phone from India gets lakhs and crores right away: “₹12,34,567.89”. More in the article [“Settings”](#settings-formats).

### What's missing {#scenario-currencies-not-yet}

- **Exchange rates from the internet.** You enter the exchange rate yourself, when you exchange. That's also protection: the app doesn't ask any service for rates, so nobody outside learns which currencies your money is in. Your data stays with you — as the [privacy policy](#settings-s-about) says too.
- **Conversion into one currency.** “Net worth” adds up only accounts in the budget currency: a euro account isn't included. On “Accounts”, a section that has an account in another currency shows no total — there's no way to add dollars to euros.
- **Budget accounts in another currency.** Why — [above](#scenario-currencies-rule).

## Overview {#overview}

The first screen at launch: is there enough money, what's urgent and what's ahead — in one feed of cards.

*The “Overview” tab in the middle at the bottom of the screen*

### Why this screen {#overview-why}

“Overview” opens first every time you launch the app. It's a morning look at your money: is everything all right, what's running low, what's ahead. You don't divide up money or edit transactions here — that's what “Budget” and “Activity” are for. Here you look and decide where to go next.

##### Is everything all right

Is there unassigned money, where's a minus, which envelopes are emptying faster than the month is going.

##### What already happened

Recent transactions, big expenses, a calendar of spending days.

##### What's ahead

Scheduled payments, goals that are short of money, and this month's spending against the usual.

Minimize the app for more than half a minute, and when you come back it opens “Overview” again. If you'd rather return to where you left off, choose that in “Settings → Appearance → Return to”.

### What's where on the screen {#overview-screen}

Tap any screenshot to open it full screen.

[“Overview” screen: header, the “Unassigned” and “Age of money” chip, “Recent transactions”, “Running out”]

1. **Settings** — the gear. All of the app's settings: sync, backups, appearance, language.

2. **Budget name** — tap it to open the list of your budgets and switch to another one.

   *How it works:*
   You can have several budgets: for example, personal and family, or an old one you don't want to delete. To create, rename or delete a budget — the “⋮” menu → “Budgets” ([below](#overview-menu)).

3. **Sync** — two arrows in a circle. Tap it — your edits go to the cloud, and edits from other devices come here.

   *How it works:*
   The number on the icon is how many edits haven't been sent yet. Right after a sync the icon flashes for 15 seconds: green — it worked, red — it didn't. Hold your finger on it, and the app tells you what happened.

   A single failure is usually the network: the subway, a cabin in the woods. If it fails four times in a row, the icon stays red with a “!” inside until it goes through. No cloud connected — the icon is its usual color, with no number.

4. **The “⋮” menu** — budgets, choosing blocks, “Hide amounts”. Explained [below](#overview-menu).

5. **“Unassigned”** — money that's in your accounts but not yet divided among envelopes. Tap it, and the app suggests how to hand it out.

   *How it works:*
   The label changes along with the number:

   - **“Unassigned”** — there's money to hand out. In the screenshot it's $300: a paycheck arrived, and the envelopes for November are already filled.
   - **“All assigned”**, in green — every dollar has a job. That's the goal.
   - **“Over-assigned”**, in red — more was put into envelopes than there is in the accounts. Take the extra out of some category on the “Budget” screen.

   You can tap it only when there's something to hand out. The “Auto-assign the budget” sheet opens: “Cover all overspending”, “Underfunded”, “Assigned last month” and other ways. Next to each is how much it would take. Nothing changes until you confirm. More about envelopes — in the article [“Budget”](#budget).

6. **“Age of money”** — how many days on average your money sits before you spend it.

   *How it works:*
   In the screenshot it's 52 days: what you spend today came to you almost two months ago. The app takes the last ten expenses and looks at which income paid for each — the oldest money gets spent first.

   Under 30 days — you live paycheck to paycheck, and any delay in pay hits the budget. Over 30 — you're spending last month's money, and the new paycheck calmly waits its turn. A minus — more was spent than there was money in the budget accounts. The hourglass on the icon says the same: full — 30 days or more, half empty — less, empty — a minus.

   Age can be calculated three ways, and from 30 days it makes sense to live on last month's money. All of that — [below](#overview-age).

7. **Blocks** — summary cards, one under another. Which to show and in what order is up to you. Each block is explained [below](#overview-blocks).

8. **“+ Transaction”** — record spending or income. Above it — the camera (a receipt by QR code) and the microphone (by voice). Scroll down, and the buttons fold into a single “+”.

   *How it works:*
   It's the same form as on the [“Activity”](#transactions) screen: every tab has a way into it.

### Age of money in detail {#overview-age}

The number on the chip answers the question: how many days did my money sit before I spent it. Your paycheck came on October 1, and on November 5 you buy bread with what's left of October's money — that money is 35 days old. The app takes the last ten expenses, finds for each which income paid for it (the oldest money first), and shows the average.

##### Under 30 days

Paycheck to paycheck: today's spending is paid from the latest paycheck, and any delay hits right away.

##### 30 or more

A month ahead: you spend last month's money. That's the method's goal — [what to do next](#overview-age-month).

##### A minus

More was spent than there was in the budget accounts.

#### Three ways to calculate {#overview-age-modes}

Age of money is a debatable metric, and there's no single correct formula for it. So the way is up to you: “Settings → Budget → Age of money”. The number changes right away — both on the chip and in the “Age of money” report.

##### Classic (YNAB FIFO) · right after installing

The YNAB way. It doesn't see transfers at all — neither to savings nor from them. Spending you had no money for weighs exactly −1 day, whether it's a hundred dollars or ten thousand.

Good if you don't touch your savings: then it's honest and matches YNAB. Bad if you live on transfers from savings: money arrives in checking, it doesn't see it, and it draws cheerful positive days. On a real budget, the accounts were $3,000 in the minus, covered by transfers from “Investments” — and Classic showed +10 days.

##### Savings-aware · closest to the truth

A transfer from savings is money entering the budget, a transfer to savings is money leaving it. Spending beyond what you had is measured in days of your income: spent a week's income beyond what you had — about minus 7 days.

The result is the average over the last 10 expenses, so one expense in the minus among covered ones is softened.

##### Savings-aware, with debt depth

The same as the previous one while the budget is in the plus. But while it's in the minus, it shows not the average but the depth of the hole: how many days of your usual income it will take to close it.

That's no longer an age but an alarm signal. Handy while you're climbing out of the minus: the number honestly shrinks with every paycheck.

##### Which to choose

Closest to the truth is **“Savings-aware”**: it counts exactly the money in your budget accounts, with everything that came from savings and went to them. Classic is honest only while you don't touch savings. Debt depth is for those who are in the minus right now and want to see how much is left. If you don't move money to savings and back and don't spend more than you have, all three show the same number.

#### Age of 30 days or more: live on last month's money {#overview-age-month}

Since your spending is already paid for by the last paycheck, this month doesn't need the current one. Give it straight to the next month: then every month starts with money ready, and you assign it in advance instead of waiting for payday.

##### How to do it

1. Record the paycheck as usual: “+ Transaction” → “Incoming”, payee, amount.
2. Tap the “Category” field. The “Where to put the income” sheet opens.
3. Choose “Income for December” — next month — instead of “Income for November”. Save.

The transaction date stays the real one — the day the money arrived. Only the budget month it shows up in changes. A paycheck you've already recorded is moved the same way: open it and change the category.

##### Which paycheck to start with

The one you haven't divided among envelopes yet. Moving it reduces this month's “unassigned” by the paycheck amount. If after that it shows a red “Over-assigned”, that money is already working this month: return the paycheck to this month and move the next one.

##### How the budget changes

- **This month** lives on what came in before. The new paycheck no longer lands in its “unassigned”.
- **Next month** gets its money in advance. On the “Budget” screen, flip forward with the “›” arrow — the paycheck is already in its “unassigned”, and you can fill the envelopes before the 1st. On “Overview”, in “Month budget” below the line — the “December budget” row.
- **On the 1st** the whole month is already paid for. Money is assigned once, at the start of the month, rather than in bits after every payment. “Assigned last month” in auto-assign does it in one tap.
- **“% income” goals** based on the month's income are known from the first day: the month's income arrived last month.
- **Reports** (“Income and expenses”, “Pay yourself”) count such a paycheck in the month it's set aside for.
- **The age of money itself** doesn't grow from this mark: it grows when you spend less than you earn. The mark locks in the reserve — next month's money won't dissolve into this one. Until its month comes, such income doesn't count toward the age.

### Five kinds of goal {#goals-types}

The kind of goal is the rule the app uses to work out “how much is needed this month”. Below, each rule with an example from the screenshot.

#### “Monthly” — for what you spend every month {#goals-monthly}

**How it's calculated** — every month you need to set aside the same amount. The goal looks at how much you put into the envelope **this month**, not at how much is left in it.

*How it works:*
“Groceries”, $400 a month. Put $400 in for November — the goal is “Reached”, even if half is already spent. Spending is exactly what this money is for. Put in $270 — “$130 to go”.

For groceries, internet, transport, subscriptions.

#### “By date” — save an amount by a certain month {#goals-bydate}

[Edit the goal sheet: Goal type By date, Amount 2,000, Save up by month March 2027]

**How it's calculated** — how much is still missing from the whole amount is divided by the remaining months, counting this one. That gives the month's contribution.

*How it works:*
“Travel & vacation”, $2,000 by March. At the start of November the envelope holds $750, so $1,250 is missing. Until March, counting November, there are five months — $250 a month. In November $150 is already set aside — the goal says “$100 to go”.

Skip a month, and the shortfall is spread over the remaining ones, so the contribution grows by itself. Put in more, and the next contributions shrink.

The bar shows something else: how much of the whole amount is already in the envelope. In the screenshot — 45%.

**What for** — a vacation, insurance once a year, holiday gifts, a spring renovation — everything that doesn't happen every month but is predictable.

#### “Balance” — keep a reserve {#goals-balance}

**How it's calculated** — the envelope should always hold this amount. However much is missing at the start of the month, that's what the goal asks for.

*How it works:*
“Rainy day fund”, $4,000. The envelope has $2,875.47 now — the goal asks for “$1,124.53 to go”. There's no deadline: top it up when you can. Took some of the reserve for a repair? The goal asks you to put it back again.

For an emergency fund, a medicine cabinet, a car repair reserve.

#### “% income” — set aside a percent of income {#goals-percent}

[Edit the goal sheet: Goal type % income, Percent of income 10%, From this month's income selected, From average income; Income for November 2,800, Goal for the month 280]

**How it's calculated** — the month's goal is your percent of income. The app shows the amount right in the sheet: “Income for November: $2,800.00 · Goal for the month: $280.00”.

**“From this month's income”** — calculated from money that has **already arrived**. The first part of your pay arrived — one goal; the second arrived — the goal grew. While there's no income in the month, the goal is zero and counts as met. **By default:** this one is selected.

**“From average income”** — the average income over the last 12 months. The goal is known from the 1st and doesn't change during the month — handy if you get paid at the end of the month.

**What for** — “set aside 10% of everything I earn” — investments, savings, donations.

#### “List” — what to buy this month {#goals-list}

A goal made of lines: write down what the category's money will go to this month — and the goal amount adds up by itself. Each month has its own list.

[Edit the goal sheet: Goal type List, List for November: Mom's birthday 100, Cake 40 — recorded 43, Colleague 35 — recorded 24 · 2 transactions; Add a line; Goal for November 175]

[List line sheet: Colleague, Planned amount 35; Recorded 24 of 35, Reduce to 24; two transactions — 20 and 4; Record transaction; Delete, Save]

**Lines** — choose “List” in “Goal type”, and the month's lines appear right in the sheet. “⊕ Add a line” — a name and an amount. At the bottom — “Goal for November”: the total of all lines. Until there are lines, “Save” is gray.

**On the category screen** — the lines live in the “Goal” card under the bar. That's where you work with them: tap a line to edit it, swipe left — “Enter”, swipe right — “Delete” (with “Undo” in the message at the bottom).

**“Enter”** — opens the regular transaction form: the planned amount (correct it to the real one), this category, the line's name in the note. Once saved, under the line in green: “✓ recorded $43.00 · Nov 12”, while the planned amount stays on the right: in the screenshot, the cake was planned at $40 and bought for $43.

*How it works:*
You can record a line again: one purchase can take several payments — tyres, then fitting. The form fills in what is left of the plan, and the line shows the total of all records and their number. The line's sheet has a link to each transaction and “Record transaction”; the “Reduce to …” button makes the plan equal to what you recorded. A recorded line can't be moved to another month.

**Move to another month** — press and hold a line to turn on selection. Check the ones you want; at the bottom — their total and “⋮”: “Move to December”, “Move to another month…”, “Delete”.

**A new month** — the list is empty: “The list for December is empty” and a “Copy lines from November · amount” button: last month's lines are copied (without the “recorded” marks), and last month doesn't change.

### Assign to goals {#goals-allocate}

Your paycheck arrived — divide it among all the goals with one button. “Budget” → “⋮” → “Assign to goals”.

[Assign to goals dialog: 230 to 3 categories — Gifts & holidays +105, Travel & vacation +100, Rainy day fund +25; 0.00 will remain; Cancel, Assign]

**What it does** — takes money from “unassigned” and puts it into the envelopes whose goals are short of something this month. First it shows the plan — how much goes to which envelope; “Assign” does it, “Cancel” changes nothing.

**If there isn't enough for everything** — goals are covered in order, top to bottom, as the categories are arranged on “Budget”, and the last one gets what's left. In the screenshot, $230 was enough for “Gifts & holidays” and “Travel & vacation” in full, while “Rainy day fund” got $25 of $1,124.53. Keep what matters higher up — the order is changed on “Budget”: “⋮” → “Change the order”.

**If there's nothing to assign** — “unassigned” is empty — the app says so. No goals — it tells you where to set them. All goals are already met — it shows for each how much is set aside and how much is needed.

**The same in auto-assign** — tap “unassigned” in the “Budget” header — the “Underfunded” mode does the same thing. And the category amount calculator on “Budget” has a “To goal: …” button — it fills in exactly what's missing.

### The summary: where the numbers come from {#category-stats}

The summary's four rows add up to one formula:

**“From …”** + **“Planned”** − **“Spent”** = **“Available”**

##### A plus carries over to the next month, a minus doesn't

Didn't spend it all? The rest stays in the envelope and shows up next month in the “From …” row. Spent more than there was? This month “Available” is red. When the month turns over, the minus is reset: next month “From …” will be 0.

*How it works:*
Where the reset minus goes depends on how you paid. With cash or a debit card, it reduces next month's “unassigned”. With a credit card, it became card debt. More in the article [“Budget”](#budget).

##### Percent of income

Under “Planned” — what percent of the month's income you set aside in this envelope. The same percent as in the “Percent of income” badge on “Budget”. No income in the month — no percent. It's counted from the “… budget” line of the “Month budget” card — without last month's leftover; why the percent can then look too high — in the article [“Budget”](#budget-income-share).

##### “≈ … a day”

“Available” is divided by the days left until the end of the month, today included, and rounded down — so a day never gets more than there is. Spent less today? Tomorrow the number grows.

## Activity {#transactions}

The whole history of your money as one feed. Every purchase, every paycheck, every “moved some from checking to savings”.

*The “Activity” tab at the bottom of the screen*

### Why this screen {#transactions-why}

The “Budget” screen answers the question “how much more can I spend”. “Activity” answers “so where did it actually go”. It's your diary: everything you've recorded is here by day, the most recent day at the top.

##### Find

“How much was that dinner in March?” — a couple of letters in the search, and there it is.

##### Fix

Wrong category, an extra zero, a duplicate entry — swipe the row and fix it.

##### Reconcile with the bank

Mark what has already gone through on the card — and you immediately see what the bank hasn't processed yet.

### What's where on the screen {#transactions-screen}

Tap any screenshot to open it full screen.

[“Activity” screen: search, menu, transaction type chips, the feed of transactions by day and the add button]

1. **Search** — finds anything by a couple of letters, an amount or dates.

   *How it works:*
   Type “whole” — the feed keeps only your trips to Whole Foods. It searches everywhere: payee, category, note and amount. “47.55” finds that amount, “milk” finds the note “Milk, eggs, bread”.

### Icons and tags in a row {#transactions-badges}

No payee — there's a dash “———” in place of the name. It's not an error: the payee just wasn't entered, and the sign of the amount shows the direction.

#### Camera — a receipt photo

A picture of a paper receipt, a price tag or anything else you'd like to keep next to the purchase.

**Don't overdo it.** Attach photos to what you'll really need, not to every loaf of bread. [Why](#transactions-attach-weight)

*How to add it and why:*
The transaction form has a “Photos and PDFs” field. Tap “Add” and choose “Take a photo” or “Pick from the gallery”. You can attach several photos.

It comes in handy when a month later you need to return something under warranty or remember what was in that $47.55 purchase. The photo is stored on the phone; for it to appear on your other devices too, turn on “Sync photos” in the sync settings.

#### QR square — an e-receipt

The code or link to the receipt that the register issued.

*How to add it and why:*
Many receipts have a QR code printed at the bottom — you can use it to find the receipt in the store's or tax service's system. The transaction form has an “E-receipt (URL or code)” field: paste the link there, or tap the scanner icon and point the camera at the QR code.

If the app knows your country's receipts, it also checks the receipt against the form: it fills in the amount if you haven't typed it yet, and if the amounts differ, it tells you and offers to fix it. The scanner doesn't change the date in the form. A receipt from an unfamiliar country is simply saved as is — and still comes in handy: in the transaction card you can copy the code and open the link.

#### “PDF” sheet — an attached file

A bill, a utility invoice, an online store receipt — anything that arrives as a file.

**Don't overdo it.** A PDF isn't compressed when added — the file is stored as is. [Why](#transactions-attach-weight)

*How to add it and why:*
The same “Photos and PDFs” field in the form: “Add” → “Pick a PDF”. The file sits next to the transaction — no more hunting for the bill in your email or “Downloads”.

Tap an attached PDF — “Open” (in a PDF app) and “Save to file” (a copy to a folder you choose) appear at the bottom.

#### Every photo has weight {#transactions-h-weight}

The database itself doesn't get fat from photos: it stores only a link to the file. But the file lives on the phone and, more importantly, goes in full into **every backup**. By default up to 19 backups are kept — 7 frequent and 12 monthly, on the phone and in the cloud.

So a single 300 KB receipt photo can take up almost 6 MB in backups, and a hundred such photos more than half a gigabyte. If “Sync photos” is on, each one also travels to the cloud and to your other devices. Same with PDFs, except they aren't compressed at all when added.

**The rule is simple:** attach what you'll need — a warranty card, a receipt for an expensive purchase, a bill for your records. Bread and milk can do without photos. And an e-receipt (QR) weighs as much as a line of text — you can save it for every purchase.

#### Fork — the amount is split into parts

One purchase — several categories. In the form it's “Split the amount” in the category list; in the transaction card the parts sit under the “Split” heading.

*How it works:*
At “Target” in the screenshot you paid $86 in one payment, but bought both food and cleaning supplies. If you recorded it all in “Groceries”, the groceries envelope would shrink by an extra $25, while “Household supplies” would stay untouched — and the budget would start lying.

So the amount is split: $61 to “Groceries”, $25 to “Household supplies”. In the feed such a transaction has a chip for each part, and its card has a “Split” section. You can split the amount in the transaction form when choosing the category.

##### Cash back at the checkout is a split too

You pay $100 by card at the store and ask the cashier for $30 in cash. The bank shows one charge for $100, but really two things happened: $70 went on groceries, and $30 just moved from your card into your wallet.

1. Record a $100 transaction from the “Checking” account and split the amount.
2. The first part — $70, category “Groceries”.
3. The second part — $30: in the category picker, scroll to the “Transfers” section and choose “Transfer: Cash”.

The feed shows a “Transfer: Cash” chip, the “Cash” account goes up by $30, and the groceries envelope shrinks by exactly $70. Record it all in “Groceries”, and the budget will think you ate $30 more, while your wallet shows less than it actually holds.

#### A line of hash signs — tags

Your labels on top of categories: “#cottage”, “#split”, “#vacation”. A transaction can have as many as you like.

*How it works:*
A category answers “what did I spend on”, and a tag answers “what for”. Milk and bread at “Whole Foods” are “Groceries”, but they were bought for the cottage. Tag them “#cottage” — and later you'll see at a glance what summer at the cottage cost, even though the money went to groceries, gas and building supplies.

“Trader Joe's” in the screenshot has two tags at once: groceries for the cottage, and paid half-and-half with a friend. A transaction can have as many tags as you like.

##### How to add a tag

1. In the transaction form, under the note, tap the “Tags” field.
2. A list opens, with recent tags at the top. Check the ones you need.
3. The tag you need doesn't exist yet? Type its name in the “Find or create a tag” line and tap “Create tag “…””.
4. Tap “Done”.

##### Where to look later

- **How much was spent under a tag** — the “Payees and tags” icon in the header (callout 2), the “Tags” tab. Tap a tag to open its screen: how much went out over all time, by category and by month, and all the transactions.
- **Find transactions** — type “#” and the start of the tag in the search: “#cott” shows everything for the cottage.
- **Who owes whom** — for a tag like “#split”, turn on “Settle up” in the “⋮” menu on its screen. The app will show how much more to collect or to return to break even.

The tag in the row isn't tappable — it's a label, not a button.

There are also: **two arrows** — a transfer between your accounts; **a little calendar with an arrow** — a transaction entered from a scheduled payment; **a sticky note** — a note: an entry without an amount, a reminder to yourself.

This is where you record everything that happens to your money: a purchase, a paycheck, a transfer from one card to another. The faster you record, the more honest the budget — so the essentials of the form can be done right at the checkout, in a few taps.

##### Open it

“+ Transaction” on any tab. From an account or category screen, the form opens with them already filled in. Even faster — a widget on the home screen.

##### The minimum

Amount, category and account. The payee, note and everything else are optional.

##### Not only by hand

The camera above the “+” reads the receipt's QR code, the microphone a phrase like “coffee 6 Fun & hobbies”. Both buttons open this same form, already filled in.

### The amount keypad {#entry-keypad}

The phone's regular keyboard is awkward for amounts: small digits, no operations, the decimal point somewhere on a second screen. The form has its own — big and with a calculator.

(+)
#### It does the math

A receipt for two, two purchases on one card — calculate right in the field.

*How it works:*
Type 120, tap “+”, type 35 and tap “=” — the amount becomes 155. “+”, “−”, “×” and “÷” all work. The chosen operation stays highlighted while you type the second number.

(.)
#### Decimal point or cents

How to type the fractional part is up to you.

*How it works:*
**With a decimal point** — like a calculator: 12, point, 50 is 12.50. While you're typing the cents, the decimal point key is highlighted.

**From cents** — like a card terminal: 1250 is 12.50. Instead of a decimal point the keypad has a “00” key: “3”, “00”, “00” is 300.00.

Switch it in “Settings → Language and formats → Amount entry”. **Right after installing:** with a decimal point.

(⌫)
#### Erase

Tap to erase the last digit. Press and hold to erase everything at once, together with an operation you've started.

(✓)
#### “Done”

Hides the keypad, revealing the other fields and the “Save” button underneath. The system Back button does the same.

(✋)
#### Which hand

The digits under your thumb.

*How it works:*
“For the right hand” — digits on the right, operations and “Done” on the left (as in the screenshot). “For the left hand” — the other way round. “Settings → Language and formats → Amount keypad”. **Right after installing:** for the right hand.

(P)
#### How to record it

A refund is money that came from the store back to the category it left from.

*How it works:*
1. “+ Transaction” → “Incoming”.
2. The refund amount. Payee — the same store. Account — where the money was returned, usually the same card.
3. Out of habit, the app puts “Income for November” in “Category” (left screenshot). Tap the “Category” field → “To an expense category…” → choose the category you bought from: “Groceries”.
4. Note — optional: “Refund: spoiled milk”. Save.

(Q)
#### What changes

The “Groceries” envelope is topped up, “unassigned” isn't.

*How it works:*
- On the “Budget” screen, “Groceries” shows “spent” $3 lower, and the balance grows by the same amount.
- “Unassigned” doesn't change: you didn't earn it, you got your own money back.
- On the category and store screens, spending is counted after refunds.
- In the “Overview” calendar, a day with only refunds is marked with an empty ring.
- Refunded to a credit card? The card's debt goes down, and with it the amount set aside for paying it in “Card payments”.

(R)
#### What if you record it as income

The budget will lie twice.

*How it works:*
“Income for November” adds $3 to “unassigned” as if you'd earned it, while “Groceries” keeps a purchase that didn't really happen. The “Income and expenses” report shows both income and expenses higher than they really are.

(S)
#### Why not delete the purchase

You can delete it if you returned everything the same day.

*How it works:*
If the purchase has already cleared the bank or been reconciled, or you returned only part of it, record the refund as a separate transaction. The bank statement will have two lines, the purchase and the refund — let the app have two as well: the reconciliation will match.

Money a friend paid you back for their half of a shared purchase is recorded the same way: “Incoming”, from the friend, to the purchase's category.

## Voice input {#voice}

Say the purchase in one phrase — “Whole Foods 70 Groceries” — and the app opens the form already filled in. Check it and save.

*the green microphone button above “+ Transaction”*

### Why {#voice-why}

At the checkout your hands are full, but you need to record the purchase right away, while you remember. Typing the amount, choosing the payee, category and account — that's five taps. By voice — one phrase.

Voice doesn't record anything by itself. It fills in the transaction form, and you're the one who saves it — so a recognition mistake never slips into the budget unnoticed.

The microphone button is green, above “+ Transaction” on “Overview”, in “Activity”, and on the account and category screens.

### The first time {#voice-first}

[Voice input sheet: microphone, Say the amount and your own names: category, store, account; For example: “70 Groceries”; What is recognised goes into the form — check and fix it there; Cancel and Speak buttons]

1. **What to say** — the amount and your own names: category, store, account.

2. **Example** — a phrase from your own budget: your most frequent category and a typical amount. Repeat it — and it will work.

3. **Where it goes** — into the transaction form, where you can check and fix everything.

4. **“Speak”** — turns on the microphone. The first time, the sheet doesn't turn it on by itself: read what to say first. After that, the microphone turns on as soon as you open the sheet.

The app asks for microphone permission — allow it. Speak, then tap “Done” or just stop talking: the recognized phrase appears on the sheet, and the form opens.

### What to say {#voice-say}

Start with the amount and category, then add the rest. The example under the microphone grows with you: two successful phrases in a row — and it shows the next step.

|---|---|
| “70 Groceries” | Amount and category. |
| “Whole Foods 70 Groceries” | Plus the payee. |
| “Whole Foods 70 Groceries note Food” | Plus a note: everything after the word “note”. |
| “Whole Foods 70 Groceries from account Checking” | Plus the account you paid from. |
| “transfer 100 from account Checking to account Visa” | A transfer between accounts. |
| “income 1400 Employer to account Checking” | Income and the account the money went to. |

##### Words the app understands

- **“transfer”** — a transfer between accounts; **“income”** — money came in. Without these words, the phrase is an expense.
- **“from account …”** or **“from …”** — which account it's from; **“to account …”** or **“to …”** — which account it goes to. You can leave out the word “account”.
- **“note”** — everything after this word becomes the note. So say it last.
- **“category …”** — if a category name sounds like a store name and the app mixes them up.

##### Amount

In digits or words — recognition turns “two hundred fifty” into 250 by itself. Cents go after a decimal point: “25.50”. If the phrase has several numbers, the amount is the first one.

### How names are recognized {#voice-names}

The app looks for **your** names in the phrase: payees, categories and accounts from this budget. It doesn't create new ones — name a store that isn't in the budget yet, and the “Payee” field stays empty.

##### The payee brings the category with it

Once the payee is recognized, the category and note fill in from its past transactions, as if you'd picked it in the form by hand. Saying “Whole Foods 70” is enough — the “Groceries” category fills in by itself.

##### How many words are needed

For a payee, one word from the name is enough. A category with several words needs at least two of them — so that “Health & medical” isn't confused with “Health”. A name said in full is always recognized.

*Why the example comes from your budget:*
An example with made-up names (“Chase 100 groceries Home Depot”) would teach a phrase that doesn't exist in your budget: repeat it, and you'd get an empty form. So the example is always built from your own data — your most frequent purchase, your accounts, a recent note.

### Didn't get it all — “To inbox” {#voice-inbox}

Voice recognized the amount, but not the category or store. No time to sort it out at the checkout, and you don't want to lose the purchase. That's what the “Inbox” is for.

##### Record it now, sort it out at home

1. Turn on “Settings → Data → Inbox” once.
2. At the checkout, say the phrase as usual. At the end, add “note” and a couple of words about the purchase — for example, “note pharmacy near home”. The note is saved even if the store and category weren't recognized.
3. Something's missing in the form? Instead of “Save” there's a “To inbox” button, and on the amount keypad too. Tap it — the purchase is recorded and marked “Sort”.
4. At home, tap the strip above the navigation or the notice on “Overview”. The form opens these purchases one by one, in the order you recorded them: “Inbox · 1 of 3”. Choose a category and account — “Save”.

The amount, date and everything voice managed to recognize are already in the transaction — with them and the note, it's easy to remember the purchase. If the account was recognized, the purchase has already reduced its balance, and on “Budget” it sits in the “No category” row until you sort it. If the account wasn't recognized, the purchase waits outside the budget and enters it once you choose the account.

*What doesn't go to the “Inbox”:*
A transfer between accounts and a split amount: they need both accounts or all the parts at once. It won't go without an amount either: if voice didn't catch the number, type it in the form. More about the “Inbox” in the article [“Transaction form”](#entry-inbox).

### If it didn't understand {#voice-fail}

| On the sheet | What happened |
|---|---|
| “Did not catch anything — please try again” | The phone didn't recognize a single word. Speak closer to the microphone and tap “Try again”. |
| “Caught neither an amount nor any of your names” | The phrase was recognized, but it has no number and no familiar name. The form won't open — look at the example and tap “Try again”. |
| “No microphone access, or recognition is unavailable…” | The app wasn't given the microphone, or the phone has no speech recognition. Check the app's permissions in the phone settings. |

If nothing was recognized, the example on the sheet becomes one step simpler. If a name was recognized but not the amount, the example stays the same: most likely the phrase just got cut off.

### Language {#voice-language}

The app listens in the interface language — the one chosen in “Settings”, “Appearance” section → “Language and formats” → “Interface language”. More in the article [“Settings”](#settings-formats). Under the example it says which language it's listening in.

##### “The interface language is not installed for speech recognition — listening in …”

The phone has no recognition model for this language, so the app listens in the phone's language. Add the language in the phone's speech recognition settings — and the line goes away.

##### The words “transfer”, “income”, “note” — in your language

Each language has its own keywords: in Ukrainian “переказ”, “дохід”, “нотатка”, in Russian “перевод”, “доход”, “заметка”. The example under the microphone always shows the words of your language.

## Receipt scanner {#receipt}

Point the camera at the receipt's QR code — the app fills in the amount and date, and recognizes a store you've seen before by its register number, filling in the payee, category and account.

*the purple QR code button above “+ Transaction”*

### Why {#receipt-why}

Receipts pile up in your pocket, and in the evening you have to remember where, how much and when. The scanner takes the amount and date from the receipt — no typos in the numbers, and the real day of the purchase, even if you enter the receipt the next day. The receipt is saved in the transaction as an e-receipt: you can throw away the paper one.

### Where the button is {#receipt-where}

The purple button with a QR code sits above “+ Transaction” on “Overview”, in “Activity”, and on the account and category screens.

It shows only if the app knows your country's receipts:

| Country | When the button shows |
|---|---|
| Ukraine | Hryvnia in the budget or on any account, or the phone was bought in Ukraine (region UA). |
| Kazakhstan | Tenge in the budget or on an account, or the phone region is KZ. |
| Russia | Ruble in the budget or on an account. |
| Portugal | The phone region is PT, or the app language is Portuguese and there are euros in the budget or on an account. |

*Why by currency and not by language:*
A language says nothing about the country you shop in: people write in Russian both in Ukraine and in Kazakhstan, and the receipts there are different. The currency, on the other hand, you chose yourself — and it tells where you pay.

In other countries there's no button. But you can save the code from any receipt in a transaction — with the scanner icon in the form's “E-receipt” field ([below](#receipt-form)). And to teach the app to read your country's receipts, send us a few — [how](#receipt-your-country).

### How to scan {#receipt-how}

[The app learns from your receipts dialog: the QR code holds only the date, amount and register number; Cancel and Scan buttons]

1. Tap the purple button.
2. The first three times, the app explains how it learns from receipts (screenshot). Tap “Scan”. Once it has recognized a store, it won't explain again.
3. The camera opens: “Point the camera at the QR code on the receipt”. The code is read by itself, you don't need to tap anything.
4. The transaction form opens with the amount, date and receipt. Check it and tap “Save”.

Is the receipt a photo or a PDF on your phone? Under the camera — “Pick from the gallery” and “Pick a PDF”.

### What comes from the receipt {#receipt-what}

A receipt's QR code holds no store name and no list of purchases — only the amount, the date and time, the receipt number and the register number. So:

| Form field | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| Amount | From the receipt. |
| Date | From the receipt: the day of the purchase, not today. |
| “E-receipt” | The code from the receipt itself. You can open and copy the receipt with it. |
| Payee | By register number: whoever you chose for earlier receipts from this register. |
| Category and note | By payee — as if you'd picked it in the form by hand. |
| Account | The one you paid with at this store last time. |

*A register means the same store:*
A register always belongs to one store. At a supermarket you end up in one line or another — with Ukrainian receipts the app also recognizes a neighboring register of the same store. If neighboring registers lead to different payees, the app stays quiet: better to pick the store yourself than to get the wrong one.

### A new store {#receipt-new-shop}

For the first receipt from a new store, the app fills in only the amount and date — and opens the payee picker right away.

[Payee sheet over the form: search, transfers, payees]

[The form after choosing: 713.00, Payee Whole Foods, Category Groceries, Account empty, Date November 9, E-receipt with a link to the receipt]

1. Find the store in the list or type a new name.
2. The category fills in by itself if you've bought from this payee before.
3. Choose the account: you haven't paid at this store yet, so the app doesn't know it.
4. “Save”.

### Repeats and errors {#receipt-problems}

| Message | What happened |
|---|---|
| “This receipt is already entered” | This same receipt is already in a transaction — the date and amount are in the dialog. “Edit” opens that transaction, “Add anyway” — a new one, “Cancel” — nothing. |
| “This is not a receipt the app knows” | The QR code isn't from a receipt, or it's from another country's receipt. The form won't open. |
| “There is no amount in this receipt” | The code was read, but there's no amount in it. Enter the purchase with “+ Transaction”. |

Some register services print a link to their own website on the receipt, with no amount or date — the app can't read such a receipt.

### A receipt from a file: “Share” {#receipt-share}

The receipt came by email, Viber or in your bank's app as an image or a PDF? Tap “Share” there and choose “My Budget”.

- A familiar receipt is found — the form opens as it does after the camera, and the app asks “Attach the file to the transaction?”. The e-receipt is added either way; attach the file if you want to see the receipt itself.
- There's no receipt in the file, or it's an unfamiliar one — the app says why and offers “Fill in manually”: the form opens with the file already attached.

### A receipt in the form {#receipt-form}

In the transaction form, under “Additional”, there's an “E-receipt” field with a scanner icon. It works with **any** QR code, in any country: it puts whatever the code says into the field.

If the receipt is a familiar one, the app checks it against the form: it fills in the amount if there isn't one yet, and if the amounts differ, it shows the line “The receipt has a different amount: …” and offers “Fix”. A receipt doesn't change the date in an open form. More in the article [“Transaction form”](#entry).

A saved receipt shows as an icon in the transaction row, and in the transaction card as “Copy” and “Open” buttons. More in the article [“Activity”](#transactions).

### Teach the app your country's receipts {#receipt-your-country}

The app reads a receipt without the internet: everything it needs — the amount and date — has to be in the QR code itself. In many countries that's the case: a fiscal receipt's QR code holds the amount, date and register number. If that's true in your country too, we can add its receipts.

##### Send 5–10 receipts

1. Take receipts from different stores: a supermarket chain, a pharmacy, a gas station, a small shop. E-receipts — a PDF or an image from an email — work too.
2. Photograph each so that the QR code and the total are clearly visible.
3. Send them to “Telegram chat” or by email with “Suggest an improvement” — both in “Settings”, “Help & contact” section. Mention your country.

Several receipts are needed to make sure the code is built the same way across different registers and chains. One receipt can't tell you that.

*What to cover in the photo:*
We need only the QR code, the amount and the date. The last digits of your card, the cashier's name, a loyalty card and anything else personal can be covered with a finger or blacked out — it doesn't affect recognition.

*When it won't work:*
If the receipt's QR code holds only a link to a website — no amount and no date — the data is on that website. The app doesn't go there: it promises to work without a server and without registration. Such receipts stay without the scanner, but you can always save their code in the “E-receipt” field.

## Scheduled payments {#scheduled}

Rent, subscriptions, your paycheck — everything that repeats. The app remembers due dates, reminds you of them and fills in the amount and category itself. Money in the budget moves only when you confirm the payment.

*“Settings” → “Planning” → “Scheduled payments”*

### Why this screen {#scheduled-why}

Half of a month's spending is known in advance: rent on the 1st, internet on the 5th, a subscription on the 11th, a paycheck twice a month. A scheduled payment is such a transaction, written down once, ahead of time.

##### Don't forget

The app reminds you of the due date with a notification — on the day of the payment or in advance, as you set it up.

##### Don't type it by hand

The due date has come — tap “Confirm”, and the transaction records itself: amount, payee, category, account.

##### See what's ahead

Upcoming payments are on “Overview” in the “What’s ahead” block and in the “Calendar”. You see what money is already promised to.

A scheduled payment isn't spending yet. Until you confirm it, account and envelope balances don't change: it only reminds and suggests.

### What's where on the screen {#scheduled-screen}

All scheduled payments in one list, the nearest due date at the top. Tap any screenshot to open it full screen.

[Scheduled payments screen: Employer +1,400 twice a month, Landlord −1,200 monthly, City Utilities, Spotify, Netflix, a dash −800 yearly with a red flag and the note Car insurance, Payment button]

1. **Row** — one repeating payment. It's laid out like a transaction row: who and how much, category and account, how it repeats and when it's next due.

   *How it works:*
   The first line is the payee and the signed amount: “−” means money will leave, “+” means it will arrive. The second is the category and account. The third is the frequency and the nearest due date.

   A payment without a payee shows a dash “———” instead of a name, and the note sits on a fourth line — in the screenshot, “Car insurance”.

   The list is sorted by the nearest due date. Payments whose due date has already passed are here too, at the top: they're waiting for confirmation — more on that [below](#scheduled-due).

2. **Frequency and date** — on the left how it repeats, on the right the next due date. After each due date is entered or skipped, the date moves on to the next one by itself.

3. **Flag** — the same colored marker as on a transaction. When the payment is entered, the flag passes to the transaction.

4. **“+ Payment”** — a new scheduled payment. The steps are [below](#scheduled-new).

While there are no scheduled payments, the screen is empty: “No scheduled payments” and the hint “Rent, subscriptions, salary — add one with the Payment button”.

### A new scheduled payment {#scheduled-new}

##### How to create a payment

1. “Settings” → “Planning” → “Scheduled payments”.
2. Tap “+ Payment” at the bottom right. “New scheduled payment” opens.
3. Type the amount and tap “Done”.
4. Choose the payee, category and account.
5. “Date” — the nearest due date.
6. “Repeat” — how often it repeats.
7. Optionally — tags and “Additional”.
8. “Save”.

##### A scheduled transfer

A credit card payment, a loan installment, topping up a savings account — that's a transfer between your own accounts. In the “Payee” field, choose an account in the “Transfers” section, and the direction — “Outgoing” or “Incoming”, as with a regular transfer.

*A transfer means two accounts:*
- The payment shows on **both** accounts' screens, in the “What’s ahead” block: as a minus on checking, as a plus on the loan.
- On the confirmation screen, in the reminder and in the weekly summary it's named by its route: “Checking → Loan”.
- “Confirm” records the transfer on both accounts at once, as the form does.
- Accounts in different currencies — “Credited” and “Rate” appear under the payee: enter the rate right away, when creating it. When the due date comes, “Confirm” opens the form with these numbers — check the day's rate and save.
- A transfer to an off-budget account (a loan, a deposit) takes money out of an envelope — the app asks for a category, as with a regular transfer. A transfer between budget accounts (checking → credit card) doesn't need a category.
- In the “Calendar” on “Overview”, a credit card payment isn't shaded as spending: the money stayed in the budget. A payment on an off-budget loan is shaded.

A split amount: when you save, the app says “Scheduled splits are not supported yet”.

### Earlier or later than due {#scheduled-early-late}

##### The paycheck came 2 days early

2. Swipe the “Employer” row left — “Enter” appears. Tap it.
3. The transaction form opens: amount, payee, category, account, tags — from the payment, date — today. Money didn't arrive today? Tap the “Date” field and pick the day.
4. “Save”.

The paycheck is recorded on the day it arrived. The due date is closed: on its day the app won't remind you or ask you to confirm. The next due date follows the schedule: twice a month — the next of the two days, monthly — a month after the due date, not after the day you recorded it.

##### Rent was paid a day late

The due date has already passed — the payment is waiting on the confirmation screen.

1. Tap the “Scheduled payments: … awaiting confirmation” banner on “Overview”.
2. In the payment card, tap “›” — the entry date moves a day later. Tap the date itself to open a calendar.
3. “Confirm”.

The transaction is recorded on the day you paid. The next due date stays on schedule — the 1st, not the 2nd.

##### The amount is different this time

Utilities are different every month. The due date has come — “Edit” in the card on the confirmation screen; not yet — “Enter” in the list. Correct the amount in the form and tap “Save”: the transaction is recorded with the new amount, and the scheduled payment keeps the old one.

## Cloud and backups {#sync}

How not to lose your budget, and how to keep it on several devices.

*“Settings” → “Sync” section*

### Why all this {#sync-why}

The budget lives on your phone. This section of the settings answers three questions.

**“My phone fell in the lake”** — three years of budget shouldn't sink along with it. [Backups](#sync-backups) and the cloud help: a new phone [loads the budget from the cloud](#sync-restore).

**“We keep the budget together”** — or it's just you, but with a phone and a computer. All devices look at one cloud and exchange edits: that's [sync](#sync-sync).

**“I messed everything up yesterday”** — deleted the wrong account, imported the wrong file. The app makes [backups on a schedule](#sync-backups) by itself, and you can go back to them.

**Where to start.** Three steps are enough:

1. [Connect a cloud](#sync-auth) — Google Drive, Dropbox or WebDAV (Nextcloud, ownCloud, pCloud, your own server).
2. [Set a password](#sync-password) if you don't want anyone else to be able to read your data in the cloud. Write it down.
3. That's it. Sync and backups run by themselves from then on: every time you open the app.

### Authorization: connect a cloud {#sync-auth}

This is where you sign in to a cloud. There are three, and you can connect one or several. In the screenshot Dropbox is already connected, while Google Drive and WebDAV aren't yet: that way you see both kinds of block.

[Authorization screen: Cloud encryption, Google Drive with a Sign in with Google button, Dropbox with a connected account, WebDAV with address, username and password fields]

(1)
**Cloud encryption** — the same block as on the “Sync” and “Backups” screens. The password is set once — explained [below](#sync-password).

(2)
**Google Drive** — “Sign in with Google” — and choose an account. The budget goes into a “My budget” folder on your Google Drive.

*How it works:*
After signing in, an account plate replaces the button: name, email, time of the last sync, and “Sign out”. On Google Drive the app sees only what it created itself: other files aren't available to it.

(3)
**Dropbox** — “Sign in with Dropbox” opens the Dropbox sign-in page in your browser. You can sign in there in two ways: with your Dropbox email and password, or with the “Continue with Google” button. Allow access — and the browser brings you back to the app. The budget goes into a “My budget” folder.

*Why your Dropbox email and password are better:*
Signing in with Google ties Dropbox to the Google account you used. A second device, or a second person with a different Google account, can easily end up in a different Dropbox that doesn't have your budget. A Dropbox email and password work the same on any phone or computer, for anyone you keep the budget with.

*A fallback, if you weren't brought back to the app:*
The main sign-in — coming back from the browser — is usually enough. If the browser never brings you back to the app, expand “Trouble signing in?” → “Sign in with a code (if you were not returned to the app)”: Dropbox shows a code, paste it into the app's dialog.

The screenshot shows a connected Dropbox: an account plate with “Sign out” and “Sync now”. “Refresh the Dropbox token” there is a fallback too: only for when sign-in suddenly stops working.

(4)
**WebDAV** — your own server or a cloud that speaks WebDAV: Nextcloud, ownCloud, pCloud, Yandex Disk and others. You need an address, a username and a password — no need to sign in to a personal account.

*How it works:*
If the cloud has two-factor protection turned on, the server won't accept your regular password: create an “app password” in the cloud's settings and enter that. Start the address with `https://` — with `http://` the username and password travel as plain text, and the app warns you about it.

**The main rule: one cloud account for all devices**

The phone, tablet and computer that keep one budget sign in to the cloud with **the same** account. Otherwise each device writes to its own cloud, and they won't see each other's edits.

*What if our family has different Google accounts?:*
Create a **separate, new Google account** for the budget and sign in to the app's cloud only with it, on all devices. Don't sign in to your personal account on your wife's phone, or to hers on yours: the phone will start syncing contacts, messages and photos with that account, and two people's personal data will get mixed up. A separate budget account knows only about the budget.

**Dropbox** — sign in with your Dropbox **username and password**, not with the “Continue with Google” button. Signing in with Google ties Dropbox to the Google account you used — and on a device with a different Google account it's easy to end up in a different Dropbox that doesn't have your budget.

**WebDAV** — the same address, username and password on all devices.

### Sync {#sync-sync}

Exchanging edits between this device and the cloud, and through the cloud — with your other devices. Add a purchase on the phone — a minute later it's on the computer.

[Sync screen: Cloud encryption, status Synced (Dropbox), cloud choice Dropbox, Sync photos and Automatic cloud backup from this device switches, Sync now button]

(1)
**Cloud encryption** — a password on everything that goes to the cloud. Explained [below](#sync-password).

(2)
**Status** — how the last sync ended: “Synced (Dropbox)” in green, an error in red, with a “Report a problem” button.

(3)
**Cloud** — if several clouds are connected, choose which one to sync with. No cloud — this spot says “No clouds are connected” and tells you where to connect one.

*How it works:*
All devices sync through ONE cloud. The app also refreshes the other connected clouds every few days — in case the main one becomes unavailable.

(4)
**Sync photos** — receipt photos and PDFs go to the cloud, into the budget's folder, and appear on other devices. **Right after installing:** off.

*How it works:*
Without this switch, transactions arrive on the second device with a photo mark, but the picture itself isn't there. Every photo takes space in the cloud; if you only look at receipts on the phone where you took them, there's no need to turn it on. With a password set, photos are uploaded encrypted.

(5)
**Automatic cloud backup from this device** — every automatic backup of the budget also goes to the cloud. **Right after installing:** on.

*How it works:*
If two devices keep the budget, both make cloud backups — and you get duplicates. Keep the switch on on one device and turn it off on the others. For the cloud backup to have all receipt photos, also turn on “Sync photos”: otherwise only photos taken on this device go into the backup.

(6)
**Sync now** — sync right now. Usually you don't need to tap it — the app does it by itself, see below.

#### When the app syncs by itself {#sync-when}

**Every time you open it** — a few seconds after you return to the app. That's why you almost never need to start a sync by hand.

**Not when you minimize it** — Android doesn't let an app keep working after you've minimized it. Edits made before minimizing go up the next time you open it. Want to send them right away? Tap the sync icon on “Overview”.

**The icon on “Overview”** — at the top right. The number on it is how many edits haven't been sent to the cloud yet. Tapping it syncs now.

*How it works:*
After each sync, the icon lights up for 15 seconds: green (it worked) or red (it didn't). A single failure is usually the network: the subway, a cabin in the woods. If four syncs in a row fail, the icon stays red until the next one succeeds — that means something broke, and it's worth opening this screen. Press and hold the icon to see the error text.

The app stays quiet about automatic syncs. The “Synced” message appears only if you started the sync yourself.

#### If something goes wrong {#sync-trouble}

A card appears on the “Sync” screen: what happened and what to do. Here are the kinds.

**“The budget “…” already exists in the cloud with data”** — with the “From cloud” and “Merge” buttons under the card.

**What happened.** You connected a cloud, and it already holds a budget with **the same name** as the budget on this device — and both already have data. Usually this is a second phone: someone added accounts or a couple of purchases on it first, and only then connected the first phone's cloud. The app can't tell whether it's one budget or two different ones with the same name, so it touches nothing and waits for your answer. It asks once — on this budget's first sync.

**What to choose:**

- **This is a second device for the same budget** — tap **“From cloud”**, then “Load”. This device gets the whole budget from the cloud, and what you entered here is lost — enter it again. This is the answer you need most often.
- **You really did keep one budget on two devices separately** and want to bring it all together — tap **“Merge”**. Everything adds up: what you entered here and what's in the cloud. But accounts and categories you created on each device separately end up side by side — two “Checking” accounts, two “The Corner Cafe” payees — and you'll have to clean up the extras by hand.
- **These are two different budgets** that just have the same name — for example, yours and your wife's, both “Main budget”. Don't tap either button. Rename the budget on this device: tap the budget name in the “Overview” header → the “⋮” next to this budget → “Rename”. With the new name it gets its own folder in the cloud and syncs separately.

Not sure? Make a backup first: “Backups” → “Create a backup now” → “Device”. If the choice turns out to be wrong, you can go back to it.

**“The cloud is password-protected”** — the data in the cloud is encrypted with a password set on another device, and here it isn't set or is different. The data is intact. “Enter password” — enter the same password, and sync continues.

*How it works:*
“Password lost” is a last resort: the file in the cloud is replaced with this device's data, and the encryption is removed. Other devices' data isn't lost — their edits come back on their next sync. Before replacing, the app makes backups, and saves the old encrypted file next to them — in case you remember the password.

**“The cloud is not encrypted”** — a password is set on this device, but the data in the cloud is open: encryption was removed on another device. You decide: “Encrypt the cloud” — then the other devices will need to enter the same password — or “Remove the password here”.

**“The sync file in the cloud is unreadable”** — the file in the cloud is damaged and won't fix itself. “Repair the cloud” makes fresh backups of the budget and overwrites the cloud file with this device's data.

*How it works:*
Edits from another device that hadn't reached this one yet remain only in the damaged file — a copy of it is saved next to the backups. If the second device works, it brings its data back on its next sync.

**“Photos from the cloud were not decrypted: N”** — the data arrived, but the photos didn't: they're encrypted with a password that isn't set here or is different. Set the same password as on the other device — the photos arrive on the next sync.

**“Conflict”** — the same record was changed on two devices. The card shows how the versions differ: “Keep” keeps this device's version, “Take” takes the cloud's.

### Cloud encryption: the password {#sync-password}

The “Cloud encryption” block comes first on the “Authorization”, “Sync” and “Backups” screens. It's one and the same password.

**What it locks** — everything that goes to the cloud: backups, receipt photos and the sync file. The encryption is AES-256. Without a password, anyone with access to the cloud folder can read these files.

**How to set it** — tap “Set a password”.

*Step by step:*
1. The “Encryption password” dialog opens.
2. Enter the password in “Password” and again in “Repeat the password” — at least 4 characters.
3. Check “I have written the password down somewhere safe” — without it, “Save” can't be tapped.
4. “Save”. The block becomes “The cloud is password-protected”.

**On other devices** — set **the same** password. The password doesn't sync and goes neither to the cloud nor to the developer — it lives only on the device. With different passwords, sync stops with the “The cloud is password-protected” card.

**Forget the password, and nobody can recover the cloud backups.** Not you, not the developer, not by any means. Write it down where you keep your other important passwords.

**Change or remove it** — in the block, tap “Edit” — “Change the password” or “Turn off encryption”. Change the password on all devices: if one keeps the old one, it stops being able to read the cloud.

**Backups on the phone aren't encrypted** — they sit next to the budget's database itself, and that's open. So when a password is set, “Device” backups in the list carry a “Not encrypted” label — so you don't think everything is locked.

#### How to return the budget to a backup {#sync-back}

1. Find the backup from the day you need. Not sure which? Take the last one before things went wrong.
2. Tap the clock-with-arrow icon in its row.
3. The “Restore the data?” dialog: “The data will be restored from … The current data will be replaced.” — tap “Restore”.
4. “Restored from …” appears at the bottom. The budget is now as it was at the moment of the backup.

**If a cloud is connected, one restore isn't enough.**

A backup is restored to get rid of recent edits: deleted the wrong account, imported the wrong file. But a restore changes the budget only on this phone. The cloud still holds the budget with those very edits, and when syncing, the app combines the phone and the cloud, and the more recent edit wins. Tap “Sync now” — and everything you were getting rid of comes back from the cloud within half a minute.

For the rollback to stick, the cloud has to be replaced with the restored budget. How — below.

#### Roll back the cloud too {#sync-rollback}

The budget is on one phone — these steps are enough. The order matters.

1. **Restore the backup** — the steps above. After that, **don't tap “Sync now”**.
2. **Leave the app** with the Home button. While the app is minimized, it doesn't sync.
3. **Delete the sync file in the cloud.** Open the cloud in a browser: the “My budget” folder → the folder named after the budget → the `ynab_budget_sync.json` file. Delete only that — leave the backup and photo folders alone. In Dropbox, the “My budget” folder is inside “Apps” → “My Budget Envelopes”.
4. **Go back to the app.** A few seconds later it syncs by itself, finds no file in the cloud, and puts the restored budget there. The “Sync” screen should say “Synced”.

*Why restore first and delete the file after:*
Every time you return to the app, it syncs a few seconds later. Delete the file first, and when you come back from the browser, the app immediately puts the current budget — still with those edits — into the cloud. Then you'd have to start all over.

#### If several devices keep the budget {#sync-rollback-devices}

The second phone and the computer have those edits too. On their sync they'd put them back into the cloud, and from there — back to you. So every other device has to forget its data and take the budget from the cloud afresh — before it gets a chance to sync.

1. Roll back the budget and the cloud on one device — the steps above.
2. On the other phone, **turn on airplane mode** and only then open the app: without a network it doesn't sync.
3. “Settings” → “About” → “Reset all data”.
4. **Turn off airplane mode.** The app syncs and loads the already rolled-back budget from the cloud.

On a computer — the same: disconnect from the internet before launching the program, reset the data in settings, reconnect. Edits on these devices that hadn't reached the cloud yet are lost with the reset.

#### Backup schedule {#sync-schedule}

The app makes a backup by itself when it's due — when you open the app and when you switch to another budget. Each budget has its own schedule. There are two kinds of backup: frequent — to go back a few days, and rare — to go back months.

[Backup schedule in Custom mode: frequent backups every 5 days, keep 7; rare once a month, keep 12; How far back: frequent about 35 days, rare up to 12 months]
**“Custom”.** The same choices as “Default”.

**Default** — frequent backups every 5 days, the last 7 are kept — that's about 35 days back; rare — once a month, 12 are kept — up to a year back.

**Custom** — frequent — every day or every 2, 3, 5, 7 days, keep 3, 5, 7, 10, 14 or 30; rare — once a month or once a quarter, keep 3, 4, 6, 12 or 24 — or “Don't make”.

**“How far back”** — the app works out how many days and months back you can go with the chosen schedule.

**Warnings** — if frequent backups run out before a rare one begins, the app shows “There may be no copies between … and … days” with buttons that close the gap. Choose “Don't make” for rare ones, and it warns that there won't be backups older than a few days.

**Old backups get deleted** — if you reduce how many to keep, the extra backups are deleted at the next automatic backup — both on the phone and in the cloud. So “Save” asks first: how many will be deleted, and the date of the oldest.

**Manual backups** — kept without limit: the schedule doesn't touch them. The schedule applies to this device and doesn't sync.

**The automatic backup failed twice in a row** — a warning “Automatic backup fails” appears on “Overview” with the reason (for example, not enough space) and a “Back up now” button.

### Load a budget from the cloud {#sync-restore}

A new phone, a reinstall, or a second device. The budget sits in the cloud under its name; this screen finds it and loads it here. The “I already have a budget” option in the first-launch wizard opens the same screen.

[Load budget from cloud screen: step 1 Connect a cloud with a Connect button, step 2 Budget name]

(1)
**Connect a cloud** — the same as on the first device: the same Google Drive, Dropbox or WebDAV and **the same account**.

(2)
**Choose a budget** — the app shows the budgets in the cloud with the date of their last sync. Tap yours and “Load budget”.

*How it works:*
No list, or your budget isn't in it? Tap “Enter the name by hand” and type the name exactly as it's called on the first device: the budget name is the address of its folder in the cloud. Upper and lower case don't matter. The name is shown in the “Overview” header on the first device.

If this device already has data, the app asks again: “Replace the data with the cloud copy?”: everything that isn't in the cloud is lost.

**Password** — if a password is set on the first device, set the same one here. Without it, sync stops, and receipt photos arrive but won't open.

### Local transfer: without a cloud {#sync-local}

Move a budget between your own devices directly — as a file or over Wi-Fi. No internet and no accounts. It's a one-time transfer, not ongoing sync.

[Local transfer screen: a File block with Export and Import buttons, a WiFi block with the sending and receiving roles]

(1)
**File** — “Export” — the budget's data as a file: send it through a messenger, by email or on a flash drive. On the other device — “Import” and choose that file.

*How it works:*
Import **combines** the data from the file with what's already there, and if the same record differs, it takes the file's version. Photos don't go into the file. To move everything including photos — Wi-Fi or a [backup](#sync-backups) (“Archive with photos and PDFs”).

(2)
**WiFi (local network)** — both devices on the same Wi-Fi network. One sends the data, the other receives it and, with “Merge”, sends its own edits back. Photos move too.

*How it works:*
1. On the sending device: “Start the server”. A QR code and a 6-digit “Confirmation code” appear.
2. On the receiving one: “Scan the QR” — the code fills in both fields. Or enter the IP address and the code by hand.
3. “Receive the data”. The app asks “How to apply the data?”: “Merge” — like a regular two-way sync: this device's edits also go to the sending one, nothing is lost; “Replace completely” — this budget becomes an exact copy of the sending one, and everything unsynced here is lost. “Replace” is for a new device.
4. On the sending device — “Stop the server”.

The transfer is encrypted with the confirmation code, and the code itself never goes over the network.

### Data {#settings-s-data}

The budget's lists and exchanging data with other programs.

[Data section: Payees, Tags, Export to CSV, Import from YNAB, Inbox]

**Payees and Tags** — lists that build up by themselves as you record transactions. All about them — in the article [“Payees and tags”](#payees-tags).

**Export to CSV** — all of the current budget's transactions as a spreadsheet file — to open in Excel or Google Sheets.

*More:*
The file has everything a transaction has: date, account, payee, category and its group, amount and currency, “cleared”, note, the parts of a split amount, transfer, tags, flag, e-receipts, the budget month (for a paycheck recorded “for next month” — the month it's set aside for), and for a transfer between currencies — how much arrived, in what currency and at what rate. Only photos and PDFs aren't exported: they're files on the phone.

Column names are in English (`date`, `amount`, `tags`…) and don't depend on the app's language: that way formulas and pivot tables don't break if you change the language. Numbers in the file are always real, even if “Hide amounts” is on. The field separator follows the number format, so the table opens as columns rather than one line.

**Import from YNAB** — move a budget from YNAB 4 (a .y4backup file) or from web YNAB (a .zip export): accounts, categories and the whole history. After the import, a glossary shows what each YNAB term is called here.

**Inbox** — at the checkout, just the amount; you choose the account and category later. How it works — in the article [“Transaction form”](#entry). **Right after installing:** off.

#### The “Language and formats” screen {#settings-formats}

[Language and formats screen: Interface language, Amount format, Amount entry, Amount keypad, Date format]

**Interface language** — thirteen languages; “System default” is the phone's language. The language lives on this device and doesn't sync: the phone can be in English and the computer in German.

**Amount format** — thousands and decimal separators, which side the currency symbol goes on. By default — as is usual for the chosen language: “$1,234,567.89”.

**Amount entry** — “With a decimal point” or “From cents, like a card terminal” (1-2-5-0 means 12.50). **Right after installing:** with a decimal point.

**Amount keypad** — “For the right hand” — digits on the right, operators on the left; “For the left hand” — the other way round. **Right after installing:** for the right hand.

**Date format** — order, separator, month as a number or a word — 24 options to choose from.

#### The “Appearance” screen {#settings-appearance}

[Appearance screen: Theme, Style, Corners, Income and spending colours, Font, Density, Icons, Transaction amount colours, Flag shape, Return to]

**Theme** — “System”, “Light” or “Dark”. “System” follows the phone.

**Style** — the app's color character: “Classic”, “Neon”, “Aurora”, “Acid”, “Candy”, “Marshmallow”, “Graphite”. Each has a light and a dark version.

**Corners** — “Sharp”, “Standard” or “Soft” — the corners of cards, buttons and chips. Independent of the style.

**Income and spending colours** — “Standard” — income in green, spending in red. “Reinforced” — income in blue: for those who find it hard to tell green from red.

*More:*
About one man in twelve and one woman in two hundred see colors differently. There are three kinds:

- **deuteranopia** — weaker perception of green; the most common;
- **protanopia** — weaker perception of red;
- **tritanopia** — blue and yellow get confused; very rare.

With the first two, green and red merge into a similar brownish color, and income can't be told from spending by color. Both of these see blue well — that's why in “Reinforced” income is blue.

**What else the app does for these people — already, without any setting:**

- every amount has a “+” or “−” sign: the direction of a transaction is visible with no color at all (and right after installing, transaction amounts have no color anyway);
- overspending speaks not only with color but with an icon too: a wallet — you spent your own money, a card — you went into credit card debt;
- a sync that failed changes not only the icon's color but its drawing too — a “!” appears inside;
- the colors of report charts are chosen so that neighboring slices differ in brightness too, not just in hue.

**What “Reinforced” changes:** income in blue everywhere — in amounts, in the income form's header, in the charts and in the “Income and expenses” table; transaction flag colors are spread apart by brightness; the report charts and “Money flow” use their own color set. Each set is checked against all three kinds during development.

“Reinforced” also helps those who tell colors apart well but look at the screen in bright sunlight.

**Font** — the system font or one of seven: “Fira Sans”, “Geologica”, “IBM Plex Sans”, “Inter”, “Manrope”, “Noto Sans”, “Source Sans 3” — for the whole interface.

**Density** — “Compact”, “Standard” or “Comfortable” — the space between rows and blocks. The size of the letters doesn't change.

**Icons** — “Standard” or “Two-tone”.

**Transaction amount colours** — whether to color amounts in transaction lists: “No colour”, “Income only” or “Spending and income”.

*More:*
**Right after installing — “No colour”**, and every screenshot in this guide was taken that way. Why: red is the color of alarm, and buying bread doesn't deserve an alarm; when every row glows red, the color stops saying anything. The sign shows the direction of a transaction: “−” for spending, “+” for income. Want color? Choose “Spending and income” or “Income only”.

Category overspending, debt and sync state stay colored whatever you choose: there, color is a warning.

**Flag shape** — “Dot”, “Triangle flag” or “Wavy flag” — how the flag looks in transaction rows.

**Return to** — “Overview” — after a break of more than 30 seconds and at launch, “Overview” opens; “Where you left off” — the same screen, even with a form open. The PIN is asked in both cases.

### Help & contact {#settings-s-help}

Hints and getting in touch with the developer.

[Help & contact section: Hints, Help Center, Telegram chat, Report a problem, Suggest an improvement]

**Hints** — all of the app's hints as a list, by screen. Also “Show hints again”. One screen's hints are also opened by the “Hints” item in its “⋮” menu.

**Help Center** — articles about every screen on the website, in the app's language; this item opens the main page. One screen's article is opened by the “Help Center” item in its “⋮” menu.

**Telegram chat** — a group where questions get answered and where you can ask other users.

**Report a problem** — an email to the developer. The app and device versions are filled in by themselves; you can attach the event log from “About”.

**Suggest an improvement** — an email with an idea: what's awkward now and how you'd like it.

#### The “About” screen {#settings-about-screen}

[About screen: Version, Windows version, Share the app, Privacy policy, License, Event log, Reset all data]

**Version** — the version number; tap it for “What is new”: what changed in the latest releases.

**Windows version** — the same budget on a computer — in the Microsoft Store.

**Share the app** — send a link to the app on Google Play.

**Privacy policy and License** — documents. In short: your data is yours, and the developer has no access to it.

**Event log** — app and sync errors. Useful if you're writing about a problem.

**Reset all data** — delete all accounts, categories, transactions, scheduled payments and goals. The app asks again: “Reset all data?”. This can't be undone.

*More:*
If sync is connected, the data comes back from the cloud on the next sync. To delete it for good, disconnect the cloud or delete the sync file in it. Before resetting, make a copy in “Backups”.
